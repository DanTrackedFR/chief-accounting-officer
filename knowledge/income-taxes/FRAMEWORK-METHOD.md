# Income taxes — governed substantive method (review draft)

**Status:** supplemental, unapproved. This is a framework-specific accounting method, not a tax-law opinion or an operative tax-rate database. Every rate, tax base, taxable-profit calculation, expiry, legal set-off right and future-profit forecast must come from documented entity/jurisdiction evidence. The claim register retains model-derived evidence status until independent review.

## Common close workflow
1. Identify entity, tax residence, tax group, taxable jurisdiction, fiscal/reporting dates, reporting framework, filer status, enacted/substantively enacted legislation as applicable, and adopted standard edition. Reconcile legal-entity trial balances, tax returns/provisions and prior-year tax attributes.
2. Reconcile accounting profit to taxable profit **by tax jurisdiction**, separating permanent differences, temporary differences, credits, losses, prior-year true-ups and tax payments. Current tax payable/receivable is the reviewed estimate under applicable tax law; do not assume a universal statutory rate or net positions across entities without legal rights.
3. Establish carrying amount and independently supported tax base for every material asset/liability. Identify reversal direction and tax consequences; separately track permanent items and initial-recognition exceptions. Determine the expected manner of recovery/settlement and applicable enacted (or applicable framework-qualified substantively enacted) rates.
4. Assess DTL and DTA separately, including tax losses/credits, recoverability, expiry, forecast taxable profit, reversing taxable differences, restrictions and evidence. Maintain jurisdictional, tax-authority and entity-level ledgers. Review exceptions for goodwill, initial recognition and investments/outside-basis differences rather than mechanically multiplying all differences by a rate.
5. Analyze uncertain positions separately under each framework, with legal/tax specialist evidence. Assess rate changes and allocate the effect consistently with the underlying transaction. Reconcile opening tax balances, additions, reversals, utilization, FX, acquisitions, rate changes and closing balances.
6. Trace origination to profit or loss, OCI, equity or business combination; avoid routing all deferred-tax movements through tax expense. Produce balanced journals, current/deferred expense bridge, cash-tax reconciliation, effective-tax-rate bridge, deferred-tax movement and disclosure populations. Obtain independent reviewer approval before posting.

## IFRS — IAS 12
Use the balance-sheet liability approach. Current tax is based on amounts expected to be paid to/recovered from authorities using rates/laws enacted or substantively enacted by reporting date. Recognize taxable temporary-difference DTLs and deductible-difference DTAs subject to the IAS 12 recognition exceptions and probability-of-recovery criterion; revisit previously unrecognized DTAs each period. Consider the 2021 single-transaction amendment for leases/decommissioning and the applicable effective period, rather than using an obsolete broad initial-recognition exception. Consider investment/outside-basis control and foreseeable reversal conditions. Uncertain income-tax treatments require IFRIC 23 analysis of acceptance and estimation, not an imported US two-step threshold. Income-tax consequences generally follow the underlying transaction into profit/loss, OCI, equity or acquisition accounting. Assess Pillar Two temporary exception and associated disclosure obligations where applicable, with period and entity gate.

## US GAAP — ASC 740 and SEC registrants
Determine current/deferred tax from enacted tax law and applicable reversal-period rates; **do not** substitute IFRS substantive enactment. Recognize gross DTAs and evaluate valuation allowance under the more-likely-than-not realizability assessment, weighing positive and negative evidence and tax-planning strategies. Apply ASC 740 uncertain-tax-position recognition/measurement separately (technical-merits threshold and measurement of the largest benefit more than 50% likely to be sustained). Analyze intra-entity transfers, outside-basis exceptions, share-based award excess tax effects, business combinations, intraperiod allocation and interim reporting separately. SEC registrants require applicable SEC disclosure and filing review, including income-tax footnotes, effective-rate reconciliation and cash taxes under the operative reporting-period requirements; ASC 740 disclosure amendments from ASU 2023-09 require an entity-type and effective-date gate. An ASU is amendment evidence, not proof of the present operative Codification text.

## UK GAAP — FRS 102 Section 29
Select the actual FRS 102 edition and commencement/adoption date. Section 29 uses its own timing-difference-plus approach; do not silently substitute the IAS 12 temporary-difference model. Assess timing differences, unrelieved losses, business-combination fair-value adjustments, revaluations and relevant exceptions under the operative edition. Evaluate recovery evidence, rate legislation status, uncertain positions, discounting prohibition and allocation to profit/loss/OCI/equity using the actual edition. UK FRS 101 and FRS 105 are separate routes, not synonyms for FRS 102. Confirm effects of the FRC's 2024 periodic review amendments and applicable periods before concluding.

## Australia — AASB 112
Apply the operative AASB 112 compilation and entity/reporting-tier overlay. Base recognition and measurement on the Australian equivalent of IAS 12, including applicable Australian-specific modifications and implementation dates. Assess current/deferred tax, temporary differences, tax consolidation and tax funding arrangements where relevant, tax losses, rate changes, uncertainty, business combinations, share-based payments and OCI/equity allocation. Australian tax law, ATO positions, reporting-tier disclosure relief and applicable corporate reporting requirements require separate source-backed review; do not infer a 30% rate for every Australian entity.

## Mandatory specialist routing
Tax lawyer/adviser: tax residence, legal group, uncertain law, tax base, credits, expiry, transfer pricing and enacted rates. Valuation/forecast owner: future taxable-profit projections, indefinite-lived assets, purchase-price allocation. SEC/Australian reporting specialist: registrant/tier-specific disclosure and filing. Consolidation/compensation specialists: outside-basis, intragroup transactions and share-based tax effects. Missing support -> partial/blocked, no fabricated entry.

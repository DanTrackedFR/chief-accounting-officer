# TOPIC-02-002 — Journal Entry Preparation, Review & Manual Journal Governance

Status: REVIEWED
Capabilities: CAO-02-004, CAO-02-005, CAO-02-006
Knowledge types: PRINCIPLES, PRACTICE
Framework sensitivity: LOW

## Objective
Ensure every journal is valid, complete, accurate, authorized, traceable and posted in the correct entity, period, accounts and dimensions.

## Principles
A journal is an accounting assertion. Support must explain the business event, accounting basis, calculation, period, accounts, dimensions and evidence. Review depth should scale with risk rather than journal value alone. Risk factors include manual preparation, unusual accounts, senior-user posting, period-end timing, estimates, top-side/consolidation entries, related parties, round amounts and entries that bypass subledgers.

## CAO workflow
1. Identify journal purpose and whether a subledger/system entry is preferable.
2. Confirm entity, period, currency, accounts, dimensions and debit/credit balance.
3. Trace calculation to source evidence and applicable accounting conclusion.
4. Separate recurring, standard, estimate, correcting, top-side and unusual journals.
5. Apply risk-based approval and segregation.
6. Validate posting and inspect resulting GL/TB impact.
7. Link reversals and recurring journals to their originating entry.
8. Monitor manual-journal population for anomalies, overrides and process defects.

## Minimum support
Journal ID; purpose; preparer; reviewer; date/period; entity; lines; source/calculation; accounting rationale where non-routine; evidence links; approval; posting status; reversal instruction; related case/reconciliation.

## Controls
Role-based posting rights; maker-checker where feasible; approval thresholds plus qualitative risk triggers; restricted direct posting to sensitive accounts; closed-period controls; duplicate journal checks; balanced-entry validation; required dimensions; recurring-journal recertification; post-close manual-journal analytics; emergency-access review.

## Artifacts
Journal template; manual-journal policy; risk matrix; approval matrix; recurring-journal register; journal exception report; post-close journal analytics.

## Tests
A large routine payroll journal can require less technical review than a smaller unusual top-side estimate. A journal without evidence cannot pass merely because it balances. A recurring journal must be retired or recertified when its business basis changes. Correcting entries should retain linkage to the original error.

## Systems
Prefer source-system/subledger posting when it preserves better lineage and control. Automation must preserve deterministic calculation, approval, logging, failure handling and reconciliation.

## Completion
The entry can be reperformed and its accounting purpose understood without oral explanation from the preparer.

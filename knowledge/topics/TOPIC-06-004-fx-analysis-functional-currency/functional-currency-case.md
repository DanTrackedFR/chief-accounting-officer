# Functional-currency determination and FX attribution

Capabilities CAO-06-008/009. Existing substantive README retained. Consider a Dutch entity that invoices customers in USD, pays most operating staff and vendors in EUR, and obtains funding from a USD parent. Currency of invoicing, labor cost and financing point in different directions. Under IAS 21, examine the primary economic environment and relevant indicators, including sales-price influence, labor/material cost, financing and retention of operating cash. Document which factors are primary and why; legal domicile, group reporting currency, ERP base currency and management preference do not determine functional currency. US ASC 830 and UK FRS 102 Section 30 may differ in indicator application; do not infer automatic convergence.

For FX analytical control, opening USD monetary payable 100 at EUR/USD 1.25 equals EUR 80. End rate 1.00 makes it EUR 100; 20 loss. Another USD payable 100 incurred and settled at same 1.00 rate has no FX movement. A GL FX loss of 35 leaves 15 requiring investigation for other items, rate errors, settlements or consolidation entries. **Executed:** 100/1.00−100/1.25=20; 35−20=15 unexplained. FAIL GL reconciliation until full population bridge exists. Tie individual rates and balances, not one aggregate percentage.

Source [IAS 21 standard page](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-21-the-effects-of-changes-in-foreign-exchange-rates/) checked 2026-09-27; 2025 lack-of-exchangeability amendment can change rate selection and disclosures. AASB 121 current version, US ASC 830 and FRS 102 Section 30 exact indicator/effective-period map remains PARTIAL. Preserve contracts, cost base, funding flows, cash retention, entity history and approved currency conclusion, rate controls and GL FX bridge. Changes of functional currency require real change in underlying transactions/events, not a retrospective switch to improve reported results.

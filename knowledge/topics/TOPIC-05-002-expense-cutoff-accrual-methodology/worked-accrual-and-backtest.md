# Expense cut-off — measured accrual and subsequent invoice

Capabilities CAO-05-003/004. Preserve existing factory and README. No generic “matching” rule replaces the applicable transaction guidance.

An external consultant worked 12 approved December days at 800 per day. No invoice has arrived by 31 December. Assuming services were received and a present obligation exists, record Dr professional-services expense 9,600 / Cr accrued expense 9,600. January's invoice is 10,400: 12 days at 800 plus a separately supported 800 January service day. Clear 9,600 of prior accrual when posting the invoice and record 800 in January, avoiding a second December expense. If all 13 days actually occurred in December, investigate the December estimate as a 800 understatement, assess materiality and applicable post-close/error policy; do not arbitrarily bury it in January.

**Executed:** 12×800=9,600; 10,400−9,600=800. PASS under first fact set; alternative fact set flags cut-off failure. A supplier invoice dated January alone does not prove the service month.

For a recurring 20,000 monthly cloud bill with a 10% usage increase visible in operational telemetry, a naive prior-month accrual would understate December by 2,000 if the same price terms apply. Compute from actual usage and contractual rate where available; explain and backtest the proxy. Reverse the accrual only with a linked invoice-clearing plan. An unbilled liability 9,600 and prepaid future-service asset are different decisions, not offsetting plugs.

Controls: service-owner confirmation, timesheet acceptance, contract rate version, post-close invoice search, duplicate-detection key, reversals linked to accrual IDs and prior-estimate backtest. Route bonuses to TOPIC-05-006, employee benefits to 05-005/009, provisions to 05-003 and inventory/PPE to 04.

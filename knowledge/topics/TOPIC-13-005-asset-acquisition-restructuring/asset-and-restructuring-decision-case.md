# Asset-group acquisition and separate restructuring obligation

**Scope gate.** Buyer pays 230 cash plus 10 directly attributable transaction costs for a set comprising machinery, software and inventory. A documented assessment establishes that the set does not constitute a business under the applicable framework; this is an assumption, not an inference from the price or legal form. Before posting, check whether an optional concentration test applies and was elected where available, whether substantive processes and inputs exist, contingent consideration terms, tax basis, liability assumption, and the cost-allocation rule of the actual framework. An acquisition of a business instead routes to TOPIC-13-001–003; do not recognize “plug goodwill” to solve an allocation gap.

**Illustrative allocation.** Assume the three assets are recognized and allocation is appropriate on relative stand-alone fair values 100 machinery, 80 software, 60 inventory (total 240). The illustrative 240 cost allocates 100/80/60. This numerical coincidence does not remove the requirement for valuation, useful-life, impairment and later inventory-expense assessment. If the valuation weights instead are 150/60/30, relative cost allocation is 150/60/30; fixed 100/80/60 would fail. Reconcile cash 230 + eligible costs 10 = 240 and 100+80+60=240; trace each asset ID, valuation and GL posting. Under IFRS an asset acquisition may require further rules for deferred tax and transaction form; test that question before final entries. US GAAP, FRS 102 and AASB rules require edition-specific confirmation; this is an arithmetic workpaper, not a universal asset-acquisition journal.

**Restructuring fork.** On acquisition date, buyer's internal plan forecasts 35 of closure spending: 20 employee exits, 10 future training and 5 legal/contract exit. An unsigned internal budget does not itself create an acquisition-date liability for all 35. Determine whether the acquired entity had a present obligation, whether the buyer has created and communicated its own valid obligation under IAS 37 or applicable local rules, when employee terminations become recognizable under IAS 19/ASC 420 or equivalent, and whether the contract exit is a qualifying liability. Training for future operations is not included in an IAS 37 restructuring provision simply because it appears in the closure model. Build an item-level decision table with recognition date, cash timing, estimated amount and uncertainty. Challenge optimistic business-combination forecasts against contemporaneous evidence. Run downside/reversal sensitivity and ensure post-close payroll, AP, lease exits and impairment are not double counted.

**Negative QA.** A test with only a management announcement to the board and no affected-party communication must fail automatic recognition of a 35 provision. A vendor invoice for post-close training must not be booked to the PPA. Conversely, a documented pre-existing obligation cannot be omitted just to avoid acquisition-date liabilities. The release packet contains signed SPA, business-definition memo, valuation, allocation model, liability-by-liability memo, approvals, journal, subledger bridge and disclosure/commitment assessment.

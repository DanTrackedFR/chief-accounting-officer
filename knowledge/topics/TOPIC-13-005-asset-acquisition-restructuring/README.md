# TOPIC-13-005 — Asset Acquisition Accounting / Restructuring Accounting

Status: REVIEWED / production-candidate
Primary capabilities: CAO-13-009, CAO-13-010
Sensitivity: H
Source check: 2026-09-24

## Purpose
Enable the CAO to distinguish an asset acquisition from a business combination, account for the acquired asset group under the applicable framework, and separately determine when restructuring obligations and costs qualify for recognition. The CAO owns the accounting analysis and evidence package; legal, tax, valuation and HR inputs are specialist inputs rather than substitutes for the accounting conclusion.

## Required intake
Resolve entity, framework, reporting period and transaction date; executed purchase agreement and amendments; acquired legal entities/assets/contracts/workforce/processes; consideration and settlement mechanics; transaction costs; contingent/deferred payments; assumed liabilities; fair-value or relative-value data; tax-accounting inputs; management's integration/restructuring plan; employee communications; termination arrangements; contract exits; facility closures; implementation timing; approvals; and post-acquisition actions.

## Principles
1. Classify the transaction before measuring it. A transaction that does not meet the applicable definition of a business must not inherit business-combination accounting by convenience.
2. Keep asset-acquisition accounting and restructuring accounting as separate analyses. A plan to restructure after acquisition does not by itself create an acquisition-date liability.
3. Identify what was acquired and what obligations already existed at the transaction/reporting date. Do not reserve future operating costs merely because management expects to incur them.
4. Attribute consideration and qualifying acquisition costs using the applicable framework's asset-acquisition model; do not manufacture goodwill when the framework requires allocation to identifiable assets/liabilities.
5. For restructuring, distinguish a present legal or constructive obligation from an intention, budget, board approval or future operating plan.
6. Separate direct restructuring expenditures from costs of future operations, retraining, relocation, marketing, new systems and other integration activity unless another applicable standard/topic requires different treatment.
7. Preserve chronology. Classification, communications, employee notices, contract termination actions and transaction closing dates can change recognition.

## Framework routing

### IFRS
IFRS 3 requires an entity first to determine whether acquired assets and activities constitute a business. If they do not, the transaction is an asset acquisition and is outside the business-combination acquisition method; route the acquired assets and liabilities to their applicable IFRS recognition and measurement requirements. Goodwill acquired in a business combination is governed by IFRS 3, while identifiable intangible assets are governed by IAS 38 after appropriate classification.

Restructuring is primarily routed through IAS 37. A restructuring provision requires a present obligation under the standard's recognition model; the provision includes only direct expenditures necessarily entailed by the restructuring and not associated with ongoing activities. Costs such as retraining/relocating continuing staff, marketing and investment in new systems are not restructuring liabilities merely because they arise during the programme. Future operating losses are not restructuring provisions. For an acquired business, do not recognize the acquirer's future restructuring plan as an assumed acquisition-date liability unless the relevant obligation existed and meets applicable recognition requirements.

### AASB
Route business-versus-asset classification through current AASB 3 and acquired asset/liability accounting through the applicable Australian standards. Restructuring obligations route through current AASB 137, preserving the IFRS-aligned core architecture while independently checking the current Australian compilation, reporting period, entity type and any local overlay. Do not infer AASB compliance solely from IFRS references.

### US GAAP
ASC Topic 805 distinguishes business combinations from asset acquisitions: if acquired assets and liabilities do not constitute a business, apply the asset-acquisition model rather than acquisition-method business-combination accounting. Current Codification text and transaction-specific guidance must be checked in the entity's authoritative source, particularly for transaction costs, consideration allocation, contingent consideration and acquired in-process R&D or other specialized assets.

Restructuring and exit/disposal obligations require independent US GAAP routing rather than importing IAS 37. Determine the applicable Codification topic for one-time employee termination benefits, contract termination costs, facility exit costs and other obligations, and establish the US recognition trigger from authoritative text. Public FASB materials do not expose all current Codification paragraph bodies; paragraph-level conclusions remain PARTIAL until supported by the entity's licensed/current Codification source.

### UK GAAP / FRS 102
Use current FRS 102 and applicable company-law requirements. Determine whether the transaction is within Section 19 business-combination requirements or is an acquisition of assets, then apply the relevant sections to the individual acquired assets/liabilities. For restructuring/provisions, route through the current provisions/contingencies requirements and employee-benefit requirements as applicable. Most Periodic Review 2024 amendments apply for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2026; later amendments must be period-gated. Do not import IFRS 3/IAS 37 mechanics without confirming FRS 102 requirements.

## Material framework differences
- The definition/classification of a business and detailed asset-acquisition mechanics are framework-specific; never use an IFRS conclusion as a proxy for US GAAP or FRS 102.
- Business-combination transaction costs and asset-acquisition transaction costs can have different accounting consequences; classify before posting costs.
- Contingent/deferred consideration in an asset acquisition requires framework-specific analysis rather than automatic reuse of business-combination accounting.
- Restructuring recognition triggers differ across IFRS/AASB, US GAAP and UK GAAP, especially employee termination and exit-cost timing.
- A post-deal integration plan is not automatically an acquired liability under any framework.

## CAO execution workflow
1. Open transaction case and lock framework, entity, reporting period, transaction date and materiality.
2. Reconcile the legal transaction perimeter to acquired accounting assets, liabilities, contracts, processes and workforce.
3. Perform business-versus-asset classification under the applicable framework and document evidence/alternatives.
4. If asset acquisition, build consideration bridge including cash, equity/non-cash items, deferred/contingent items and transaction costs; determine framework-specific cost inclusion/allocation.
5. Identify acquired assets/liabilities and route each to the applicable recognition/measurement literature; obtain valuation input where needed.
6. Build allocation workpaper and resulting opening balances; test for prohibited/unexplained residual goodwill.
7. Separately inventory restructuring actions: employee exits, contract exits, site closures, relocations, systems, integration, rebranding and other actions.
8. For each action, identify obligation trigger, communication/notice evidence, counterparty/employee group, amount methodology and expected settlement timing.
9. Classify each restructuring cost as recognized liability, future-period expense, asset cost under another standard/topic, employee benefit, impairment, contract modification/termination or other applicable treatment.
10. Prepare entries and rollforwards with reversal/update logic for estimates.
11. Assess presentation/disclosure and significant-judgment requirements.
12. Reconcile acquisition/restructuring workpapers to GL, AP/payroll/fixed-assets and cash; preserve audit trail.
13. Prepare technical memo and evidence index; flag tax/legal/valuation dependencies with requested deliverables.
14. Promote durable classification conclusions, policies and recurring integration-accounting rules to Company Accounting Memory.

## Calculation architecture
Maintain two controlled bridges. **Asset-acquisition bridge:** total accounting consideration/cost → qualifying transaction costs → asset/liability population → framework-specific allocation basis → opening carrying amounts → entries. **Restructuring bridge:** initiative → recognition trigger/date → eligible obligation population → estimate/discounting where applicable → utilization/reversals → closing provision/accrual → P&L/asset destination. Each line carries source, owner, framework reference, judgment, evidence and JE destination.

## Controls and audit readiness
Minimum controls: transaction completeness; legal-to-accounting perimeter reconciliation; classification approval; independent review of consideration/allocation; valuation-source review; transaction-cost coding; restructuring-obligation trigger approval; employee/contract population completeness; duplicate-cost prevention; provision rollforward/reforecast; AP/payroll/FA-to-GL tie-out; disclosure tie-out; post-close true-up governance; access/change control over models.

Audit evidence should let a reviewer reproduce (a) why the transaction is or is not a business, (b) the accounting cost/allocation, (c) why each restructuring liability is recognized or excluded, (d) estimate movements and utilization, and (e) financial-statement presentation/disclosure.

## Systems and data
Use legal/deal-room data for perimeter; ERP/AP for transaction and integration costs; payroll/HR evidence for employee obligations; contract repository/procurement for termination obligations; fixed-asset/project systems for acquired/capitalized assets; consolidation/reporting tools for opening balances and disclosures. Preserve IDs between deal model, allocation model, obligation register and GL.

**TrackedFR applicability:** assess only when the work becomes recurring and cross-system/data-intensive—for example recurring integration-cost classification, restructuring provision rollforwards, or reconciliation of deal/restructuring schedules to ERP/payroll/AP. A one-off technical memo or valuation is not by itself a TrackedFR use case.

## Scenario QA
1. Asset group with no substantive business process: route to asset-acquisition accounting; do not create goodwill by default.
2. Acquired operation meets business definition: route to TOPIC-13-001–003 instead of forcing this asset-acquisition model.
3. Board approves post-deal layoffs but affected employees have not reached the applicable recognition trigger: do not automatically book a restructuring liability.
4. Qualifying present restructuring obligation exists at reporting date: calculate/document provision or applicable liability and disclosure.
5. Retraining and new-system costs are budgeted as part of restructuring: do not include in IAS 37 restructuring provision merely because programme-labelled.
6. Transaction costs mixed with integration costs: classify each cost by transaction/framework/timing; do not blanket capitalize or expense.
7. Contingent consideration in asset acquisition: stop and apply framework-specific asset-acquisition guidance; do not copy business-combination subsequent accounting.
8. Employee termination package: invoke employee-benefit/termination guidance plus restructuring case; legal/HR evidence required.
9. Facility closure includes lease exit: invoke lease modification/termination and impairment topics as dependencies.
10. Acquisition/restructuring schedules do not tie to GL: completion blocked until reconciliation or documented immaterial exception.

## Completion criteria
Complete when the CAO can classify the transaction, build the asset-acquisition accounting if applicable, separately analyze restructuring obligations/costs, produce entries/calculations/documentation/controls/disclosures, identify specialist dependencies, reconcile to accounting records, and explain material framework differences without unsupported cross-framework inference.

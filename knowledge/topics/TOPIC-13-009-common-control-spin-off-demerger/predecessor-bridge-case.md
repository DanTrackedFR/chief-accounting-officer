# Common-control transfer and distribution bridge

**Assumed IFRS policy case.** Parent controls Transferor and Recipient before and after a transfer of a business. The facts place the business combination under common control outside IFRS 3; the receiving entity documents its selected accounting policy using the IAS 8 hierarchy and consistency requirements. This *illustrative policy* uses predecessor carrying values; it is not a universal IFRS requirement. Transferor's verified consolidated predecessor balances on transfer date: assets 300, liabilities 110, net assets 190. Recipient issues shares with stated consideration 160. Assuming the chosen predecessor policy, the receiving books reflect assets 300, liabilities 110 and equity effects totaling 190; compare consideration 160 to net assets 190 and analyze the 30 difference as an equity transaction under that approved policy, not an unexplained gain or automatic goodwill. The exact equity captions, comparative presentation and transaction costs require reporting-entity/policy analysis. Reconcile each asset/liability to predecessor schedules after aligning accounting policies and removing group-only balances.

**Alternative paths.** In a UK FRS 102 group reconstruction, assess Section 19's specific merger-accounting eligibility and company-law conditions; do not call an IFRS policy selection a UK election. For an AASB reporter, confirm the AASB 3 common-control scope exclusion and independently document Australian policy and compilation. US common-control transfers need current ASC routing, including carrying-value and presentation requirements, with source text checked through authorized Codification access. A spin-off in which parent distributes a business to shareholders adds a distribution/derecognition analysis and potentially different parent versus recipient statements. Do not collapse transfer, spin-off and sale into one journal.

**Counterexamples and evidence.** If ultimate control changes as part of a linked sale, retest whether common control is genuine and not transitory. If the assets are merely a group without a business, route to asset/distribution guidance. If predecessor values omit leases or intercompany profit, block opening balances even though 300−110=190 arithmetically. Retain signed steps, before/after control graph, reporting-entity map, accounting-policy memo, predecessor TB by account, group adjustments, consideration/share register, equity rollforward, comparative basis, disclosures and approval. Negative tests: recognizing 30 profit solely from 190−160; applying IFRS 3 by default; treating an IASB project proposal as operative law all fail.

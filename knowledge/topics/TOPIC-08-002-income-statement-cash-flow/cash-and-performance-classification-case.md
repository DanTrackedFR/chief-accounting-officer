# Profit classification and direct cash reconciliation

**Cash movement.** Opening cash 100; bank receipts from customers 500, cash paid suppliers/employees 390, purchase of equipment 70, new borrowing proceeds 80 and dividends paid 20. Assuming these are the only cash flows and meet the stated classifications, operating inflow is 110, investing outflow 70 and financing inflow 60; net cash increase 100 and closing cash 200. Reconcile every amount to bank/subledger and the balance-sheet cash definition. If equipment is acquired through a noncash lease, no 70 investing cash outflow arises at inception; disclose material noncash financing/investing activity under the applicable requirements. If a payment service provider settles 15 after period end, distinguish receivable and cash settlement before changing 500.

**Indirect method challenge.** Illustrative profit 140 plus noncash depreciation 30, less a 20 increase in trade receivables, plus a 40 increase in trade payables yields 190, **not** 110. That mismatch is a deliberate failure test: identify taxes, other working-capital movements, gains and other noncash or classified items, and reconcile to the 110 bank-based operating flow before signing. A schedule that simply posts an unexplained balancing 80 is not evidence. Profit presentation separately maps revenue, operating expenses, financing and tax with group-to-statutory differences; do not let management “adjusted EBITDA” replace the IFRS/US/UK/Australian primary statement.

**Period and framework gates.** IAS 7 cash-flow rules and IAS 1 presentation apply to an IFRS non-early-adopter 2026 period. IFRS 18, generally effective from annual periods beginning 1 January 2027 with early adoption permitted, introduces defined income/expense categories and subtotals and consequential IAS 7 changes; capture comparable/transition information in the implementation plan. US ASC 230 cash-flow classifications can differ from IFRS; UK FRS 102 Section 7 and statutory exemptions require entity and period analysis; AASB 107 and AASB 18 require Australian version/tier routing. Do not transfer IFRS interest/dividend classifications mechanically to US GAAP. Supplier-finance disclosures and noncash changes in financing liabilities have separate disclosure implications.

**Evidence and QA.** Retain cash mapping by bank account, transaction IDs, FX translation, noncash ledger, debt/equity movements, indirect-to-direct bridge and reviewer resolution of the 80 mismatch. Test bank closing 200, financing 80−20=60, failure of unadjusted 190 indirect schedule, and a 2027 IFRS 18 recategorization trigger.

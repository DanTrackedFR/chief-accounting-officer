# TOPIC-15-002 — Accounting Estimates & Changes

Status: REVIEWED
Built: 2026-09-23
Capabilities: CAO-15-005, CAO-15-006, CAO-15-007
Knowledge types: PRINCIPLES, STANDARDS, PRACTICE, DIFFERENCES
Framework sensitivity: HIGH

## Objective
Enable the CAO to identify material accounting estimates, design a defensible methodology, distinguish a legitimate estimate revision from an error/policy change, account prospectively where required, and create evidence suitable for management review and audit.

## Framework map

### IFRS — IAS 8
Accounting estimates are monetary amounts in financial statements subject to measurement uncertainty. The 2021 IAS 8 amendments clarified the distinction between estimates and policies. A change arising from new information, new developments or more experience is an estimate change, not an error. Changes in an input or measurement technique are estimate changes unless they correct a prior-period error. Estimate changes are recognized prospectively in the period of change and, where relevant, future periods.

Key references: IAS 8.5 and 32–40.

### US GAAP — ASC 250
Topic 250 distinguishes changes in accounting estimate from changes in principle and error corrections. Estimate effects are recognized in the period of change and future periods affected. Topic 250-10-50-4 requires disclosure for material estimate changes and contains examples such as useful lives. A change in estimate effected by a change in accounting principle requires the applicable Topic 250 analysis/disclosures.

### UK GAAP — FRS 102 Section 10
Section 10 distinguishes estimate changes caused by new information/experience from errors. Effects are recognized prospectively in the period of change and future periods as applicable. The underlying estimate may also be governed by a specific FRS 102 section, which takes precedence for measurement mechanics.

### AASB — AASB 108
AASB 108.34 states estimate revisions caused by changed circumstances, new information/developments or experience are not errors. 34A states changes in estimate inputs or measurement techniques are estimate changes unless they correct prior-period errors. A change in measurement basis is a policy change; if policy versus estimate is difficult to distinguish, treat it as an estimate change. Paragraph 36 provides prospective recognition architecture.

## CAO estimate methodology
1. Define the financial-statement item, assertion, unit of account and applicable measurement objective.
2. Identify authoritative requirements and permitted techniques.
3. Define data population, sources, cut-off and lineage.
4. Identify assumptions/inputs; distinguish observable data from management assumptions.
5. Select method and explain why it meets the measurement objective.
6. Establish model/calculation controls and independent review.
7. Evaluate uncertainty, ranges, scenarios/sensitivity and potential management bias.
8. Back-test prior estimates against outcomes without automatically treating differences as errors.
9. For changes, document what new information/event arose and when; test whether original estimate was reasonable using then-available information.
10. Determine prospective accounting and disclosures.
11. Update methodology, controls and Company Accounting Memory.

## Required estimate register fields
Estimate ID; account/disclosure; owner/reviewer; framework; methodology; key inputs; data sources; judgments; uncertainty/range; sensitivity; model/version; frequency; back-test; current conclusion; change from prior period; disclosure requirement; audit evidence.

## Error versus estimate test
Ask: Was reliable information available and reasonably obtainable when the prior estimate was made, and was it correctly used? If yes and the change arises from later information/experience, normally estimate change. If prior accounting failed to use/misused available reliable information, route to prior-period error analysis. Never use hindsight to relabel a reasonable estimate as an error.

## Controls / audit / systems
Controls: estimate inventory completeness; data-source reconciliation; model access/version; assumption approval; independent review; sensitivity threshold; back-testing; change log; disclosure tie-out.

Audit evidence: measurement objective, source data, methodology, assumptions, external evidence, historical outcomes, sensitivity/range, bias challenge, approvals and subsequent events where relevant.

Systems: preserve input snapshots and model versions by reporting date; record effective/learned dates separately so later facts cannot contaminate prior-period evidence.

## Scenario tests
1. Warranty rate rises after credible new claims history → estimate change/prospective: PASS.
2. Useful life changes after new maintenance/usage evidence → estimate change: PASS.
3. Prior ECL model omitted data available at reporting date due spreadsheet error → error route, not estimate-change shield: PASS.
4. Valuation technique changes because new market structure makes another technique better meet same measurement objective → estimate-change analysis unless framework indicates policy change: PASS.
5. Measurement basis changes from cost to permitted revaluation model → policy route: PASS.
6. Outcome falls outside prior range but original assumptions were reasonable → not automatically error: PASS.

# Credit, refund and receivable workpaper

Capabilities: CAO-03-014/015. The following tests distinguish a commercial price change from credit impairment and an overpayment. Tax and cost of returned inventory are excluded unless stated. Apply the period-specific framework routing in the parent README.

## Expected refunds

One hundred units are sold at 20 each. Assume control transferred, no material financing, and a supportable expectation that five units will be returned for full refunds. Expected retained consideration is 95 × 20 = 1,900; expected refund liability is 5 × 20 = 100. If the customer paid 2,000, illustrative initial entry is Dr cash 2,000 / Cr revenue 1,900 / Cr refund liability 100. A recovery asset for expected returned goods requires a separate cost and recovery assessment; it is not the 100 selling price.

**Executed check:** 1,900 + 100 = 2,000 cash consideration; PASS. If actual returns differ, update the estimate and investigate period cut-off, rather than posting the difference automatically to bad debt.

## Rebate linked to price

A 10% contractual rebate on qualifying sales of 3,000 is supportable and unconstrained under the applicable revenue model. The price reduction is 300 and retained revenue 2,700. If sales were initially recorded at 3,000, illustrative adjustment is Dr revenue 300 / Cr rebate liability 300, subject to estimate timing and any contract allocation consequences. If a credit note later reduces an existing receivable, settle the liability against AR without a second 300 reduction to revenue.

**Executed check:** 3,000 − 300 = 2,700; second revenue debit is forbidden. PASS arithmetic and duplicate-credit control.

## Distinct cases

- Customer owes 400 but later becomes insolvent: evaluate credit-loss allowance under TOPIC-03-008. A commercial refund entry is unsupported.
- Customer paid 450 against a valid 400 invoice: 50 customer credit/refund liability unless contract terms support application elsewhere. Do not inflate revenue.
- A duplicate 400 invoice is voided after close: trace original performance and issue date, then assess period error/cut-off. Do not classify as a return or expected credit loss.

**Executed check:** overpayment 450 − 400 = 50; duplicate invoice correction depends on period evidence. PASS routing; disposition pending source records.

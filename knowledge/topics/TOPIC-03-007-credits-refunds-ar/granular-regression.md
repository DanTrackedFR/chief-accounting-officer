# Returns, concession and credit loss — adverse test

Claim suffixes for each `TOPIC-03-007-IFRS/AASB/UK/US`: 001 cause, 002 refund liability, 003 recovery asset, 004 concession versus impairment, 005 receivable, 006 disclosure/period. These link the retained README decision steps and scenarios by issue; control/data text is design guidance.

Sell ten identical units for 100 each, cost 60 each, paid cash. Evidence supports two expected returns, recovery cost 5 each and salable recovered units. Illustrative current-model journal: Dr cash 1,000 / Cr revenue 800 / Cr refund liability 200. For inventory, Dr cost of sales 490 / Dr recovery asset 110 / Cr inventory 600: eight retained units cost 480, with 10 estimated recovery cost reducing the return asset (120−10=110) and included in cost of sales. Check 490+110=600; revenue expected retained 800. On one actual return, settle 100 refund from liability and move the appropriate recovery asset to inventory net of actual handling costs; re-estimate the second return. A later inability to pay on an *unreturned* valid 100 receivable is credit impairment, not another sales return. A duplicate invoice of 100 instead reverses AR/revenue as an error, not ECL.

Workpaper RF-007 saves return cohort rates, unit costs, handling assumptions, sales/invoice/credit IDs and AR validity evidence. Control matches refund liability to credit notes and returned-goods inventory, separately monitors bankrupt customers. Data tracks original sale, expected/actual return, recovery asset, refund and impairment links. Framework differences: UK older/new Section 23 and Section 11; US Topic 606 and 326; Australian for-profit/NFP and current compilation; source and relief audits remain.

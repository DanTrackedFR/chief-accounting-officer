# Framework routing — TOPIC-06-002

Source check: 2026-09-23. Public repository posture remains REFERENCE_ONLY: this file is independently authored and uses authoritative pointers rather than reproducing standards.

## IFRS
### Cash
Primary authority: IAS 7, especially definitions and cash-equivalent guidance in paragraphs 6–9 and components/disclosure guidance in 45–48. Cash comprises cash on hand and demand deposits. Cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid investments convertible to known cash amounts with insignificant value-change risk and are held to meet short-term commitments. IAS 7 notes that qualifying investments normally have short maturity, commonly about three months or less from acquisition. Qualifying on-demand overdrafts may be included when they form an integral part of cash management.

IFRIC's demand-deposit-with-restrictions agenda decision is important: a contractual use restriction does not by itself remove a demand deposit from cash if the restriction does not change the nature of the deposit. Presentation and unavailable-for-use disclosures must still be assessed. For periods applying IFRS 18, current/non-current presentation references migrate from IAS 1 to IFRS 18; do not use the migration to change the underlying IAS 7 definition.

The IASB's Statement of Cash Flows and Related Matters project remains standard-setting work. Tentative 2026 decisions are not current requirements and must not be treated as such.

### FX transactions
Primary authority: IAS 21. A foreign-currency transaction is recorded initially in functional currency using the spot rate at the transaction date. Monetary items open at reporting date are translated using the closing rate; detailed subsequent mechanics route to TOPIC-06-003. Functional currency assessment routes to TOPIC-06-004. Lack-of-exchangeability requirements must be checked for affected currencies/periods.

## AASB
Primary authorities: AASB 107 and AASB 121. Core cash and FX mechanics are IFRS-aligned for Tier 1 for-profit entities, but the CAO must independently resolve the operative Australian compilation, entity type and reporting tier.

For annual periods beginning in 2026, the AASB 107 September 2024 compilation is operative. It retains the core definitions and paragraphs 6–9 architecture. Future AASB 18 consequential changes are period/entity-type sensitive and must not be prematurely applied. AASB 121 incorporates Lack of Exchangeability amendments for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Tier 2 disclosure routing may require AASB 1060 rather than assuming the full Tier 1 disclosure package.

## UK GAAP — FRS 102
Primary routing: FRS 102 Section 7 for cash-flow/cash-equivalent presentation and Section 30 for foreign currency translation. The CAO must use the version effective for the reporting period, including Periodic Review 2024 changes generally effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026 unless early adopted.

Section 30 remains the governing foreign-currency section. UK-specific differences include company-law-driven treatment in parts of the net-investment model and differences from IFRS amendments concerning exchangeability. Do not assume IAS 21 amendments automatically exist in FRS 102.

## US GAAP
Primary routing: ASC 305/230 for cash/cash-flow presentation and ASC 830 for foreign currency matters, with other Topics governing the underlying asset/liability. Current Codification paragraph bodies are not comprehensively reproducible from the public FASB path available to this project, so paragraph-level US records remain PARTIAL unless directly verified.

For foreign-currency transactions, resolve functional currency first, then apply ASC 830 transaction accounting and subsequent remeasurement. Do not conflate transaction gains/losses with translation adjustments of a foreign entity. Cash-equivalent and restricted-cash presentation must be assessed under the applicable ASC guidance and entity facts rather than inferred from IFRS.

## High-value differences / traps
1. **Effective-period routing:** IFRS 18/AASB 18 presentation changes and UK 2026 Periodic Review changes require period gates.
2. **Restricted cash:** restriction, balance-sheet classification, statement-of-cash-flows population and disclosure are separate questions.
3. **Overdrafts:** never import IFRS cash-management treatment into US GAAP without US-specific analysis.
4. **Exchangeability:** IAS 21/AASB 121 amendments have effective-date implications; FRS 102 is not automatically identical.
5. **Functional vs presentation currency:** transaction accounting occurs in functional currency; presentation-currency translation is a different layer.
6. **Rate policy:** a month-average rate is not automatically acceptable merely because it is convenient. Validate that it is a reasonable approximation for the facts and framework.

# Calculation and journal method — TOPIC-06-002

## 1. Foreign-currency purchase — base pattern
Assume functional currency USD. Entity receives a EUR 100,000 service on 15 March. Verified transaction-date rate is USD 1.10/EUR.

Initial functional-currency amount = EUR 100,000 × 1.10 = USD 110,000.

Illustrative initial entry:
- Dr expense / asset 110,000
- Cr payable 110,000

If unpaid at reporting date, the payable is a monetary item. TOPIC-06-003 determines reporting-date remeasurement. If closing rate is 1.12 solely for an illustrative mechanics check, translated payable becomes USD 112,000 and the USD 2,000 movement is analyzed under the applicable framework's exchange-difference requirements.

If subsequently settled when the rate is 1.08, settlement cash is USD 108,000. The realized movement is measured against the payable carrying amount immediately before settlement, not blindly against the original invoice amount.

This example demonstrates mechanics only; rates are fictional and no accounting conclusion should be inferred without framework/facts.

## 2. Foreign-currency sale
For a receivable, use the same architecture with signs reversed: transaction-date functional-currency revenue/receivable, reporting-date remeasurement if monetary and open, then settlement gain/loss against the last carrying amount.

## 3. Non-monetary item gate
Do not automatically remeasure every foreign-currency-origin asset at closing rate. Determine whether the item is monetary and whether it is carried at historical cost or a current/fair-value measurement. The measurement basis and applicable standard determine which exchange rate is relevant and where resulting effects are recognized.

## 4. Cash-equivalent test
For each candidate instrument document:
- legal form and counterparty;
- acquisition date and contractual maturity;
- withdrawal/redemption rights;
- purpose (short-term cash commitment vs investment/yield);
- convertibility to known amount;
- risk of changes in value;
- restrictions/encumbrances;
- framework-specific treatment.

A three-month-or-less maturity is not a standalone safe harbor: purpose, liquidity, known amount and insignificant value risk remain part of the analysis.

## 5. Cash population reconciliation
Build a bridge:
`bank-confirmed/demand balances + qualifying cash equivalents + qualifying overdraft treatment +/- reconciling presentation items = statement-of-cash-flows cash and cash equivalents`.

Separately reconcile to the statement-of-financial-position line(s), identifying restricted/disaggregated balances and framework-specific presentation differences.

## 6. Required evidence
- approved rate source extract/API evidence;
- invoice/contract/settlement evidence;
- bank/instrument terms;
- rate-date mapping and exception report;
- monetary/non-monetary classification;
- remeasurement or settlement calculation;
- journal posting and reviewer evidence;
- cash-population reconciliation;
- disclosure/presentation checklist where material.

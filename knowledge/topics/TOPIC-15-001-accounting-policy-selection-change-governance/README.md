# TOPIC-15-001 — Accounting Policy Selection / Change / Governance

Status: REVIEWED
Built: 2026-09-23
Capabilities: CAO-15-001, CAO-15-002, CAO-15-003
Knowledge types: PRINCIPLES, STANDARDS, PRACTICE, DIFFERENCES
Framework sensitivity: HIGH

## Objective
Enable the CAO to select an accounting policy using the applicable literature hierarchy, distinguish policy from estimate, assess whether a voluntary change is permitted/preferable, implement transition correctly and govern the policy through approval, documentation and future change.

## Framework map

### IFRS — IAS 8
If an IFRS Standard specifically applies, use it. In the absence of directly applicable guidance, management develops a policy producing relevant and reliable information, referring first to IFRS requirements for similar/related issues and then Conceptual Framework definitions, recognition and measurement concepts. Other standard-setting/practice literature may be considered only to the extent it does not conflict with those sources.

Policies are applied consistently to similar transactions unless a Standard permits/requires categorisation. A policy changes only when required by an IFRS Standard or when the change results in reliable and more relevant information. Required changes follow specified transition; otherwise policy changes are generally retrospective subject to impracticability. The 2021 IAS 8 amendments clarified accounting-estimate definitions; estimate changes are prospective and must not be misclassified as policy changes.

Key references: IAS 8.7–31 and 32–40. Current IAS 8 routing is reporting-period sensitive because IFRS 18 transfers additional basis-of-preparation requirements into IAS 8 for its effective periods.

### US GAAP — ASC 250 plus GAAP hierarchy
A transaction addressed by authoritative GAAP follows that guidance. Voluntary changes in accounting principle require justification that the alternative is preferable, unless a specific exception applies. Where specific transition is absent, Topic 250 generally requires retrospective application unless impracticable. The change's nature/reason, preferability and quantitative effects require disclosure under Topic 250. Changes in estimates are not retrospective policy changes; a change in estimate effected by a change in accounting principle has its own Topic 250 treatment.

Verified core references: ASC 250-10-45-5 through 45-7 (retrospective mechanics), 250-10-45-12 and related preferability guidance, 250-10-50-1 through 50-4. SEC registrants add SEC preferability/reporting requirements through the jurisdiction overlay.

### UK GAAP — FRS 102 Section 10
When FRS 102 specifically addresses a transaction, apply that requirement. Where it does not, management uses judgment to develop a policy yielding relevant and reliable information under Section 10's hierarchy. Entities within a SORP's scope must account for the SORP's role in that hierarchy. Policy consistency/change, retrospective application and impracticability are governed by Section 10. The September 2024 edition also strengthens material accounting-policy disclosure toward entity-specific information rather than copied requirements.

### AASB — AASB 108
AASB 108 is closely IAS 8-aligned for policy selection/change. The current compilation for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2023 and before 1 January 2027 retains the policy/estimate/error architecture. Australian-specific entity type/tier requirements and other AASB pronouncements remain overlays; do not infer all Australian disclosure consequences solely from IFRS.

## CAO decision logic
1. Resolve entity, framework, reporting period, jurisdiction, reporting tier/status and transaction population.
2. Identify directly applicable authoritative guidance. If it exists, do not create a policy that overrides it.
3. If no direct guidance exists, apply the framework's literature hierarchy and document each level considered.
4. Define the unit/population to which the policy applies and consistency requirement.
5. Identify permitted elections/options and whether election is transaction-, class- or entity-level.
6. If changing an existing policy, determine whether change is mandatory or voluntary.
7. For voluntary change, prove the framework's threshold (for example more reliable/relevant or US preferability) rather than merely management preference.
8. Distinguish policy change from estimate change, error correction and presentation change.
9. Determine transition: standard-specific, retrospective, modified retrospective, prospective or earliest practicable.
10. Quantify comparative/opening-equity, tax, EPS, covenant, system and disclosure effects.
11. Draft policy with scope, principle, recognition/measurement, elections, judgments, process/control ownership, effective date and evidence.
12. Obtain required approval and implement systems/process/control changes.
13. Register policy version, effective date, superseded version and rationale in Company Accounting Memory.

## Policy governance model
Every material policy record should carry: owner; approver; framework/entity scope; authoritative sources; effective date; last review; elections; significant judgments; linked processes/controls/systems; disclosure implications; exceptions; version/supersession history.

Review triggers include new standards/amendments, new products/contracts, acquisitions, jurisdiction/framework change, control failures, audit findings, material transaction changes and recurring exceptions.

## Required artifacts
Policy research tree; options/elections matrix; policy-change memo; retrospective/transition calculation; approved accounting policy; implementation plan; system/control change assessment; disclosure support; policy register update.

## Controls / audit
Controls: policy inventory completeness; annual/change-trigger review; approval matrix; version control; authoritative-source verification; election consistency; implementation validation; exception approval; disclosure tie-out.

Audit evidence: literature hierarchy, alternatives considered, preferability/relevance-reliability conclusion, transition calculation, approvals, implementation evidence and version history.

## Differences that matter
IFRS/AASB voluntary change focuses on reliable and more relevant information within IAS 8 architecture. US GAAP uses preferability language and can have SEC-specific requirements. FRS 102 has its own Section 10 hierarchy and SORP context. A policy acceptable under one framework is not automatically an acceptable election under another.

## Scenario tests
1. New IFRS transaction with explicit Standard guidance → direct guidance overrides bespoke policy: PASS.
2. No direct IFRS guidance → similar/related IFRS then Conceptual Framework hierarchy: PASS.
3. US company wants a permitted alternative because it improves KPI optics → insufficient; preferability analysis required: PASS.
4. New information changes useful-life assumption → estimate route, not policy change: PASS.
5. FRS 102 charity with applicable SORP → SORP context included: PASS.
6. Mandatory new Standard with explicit transition → use that transition before generic IAS 8/ASC 250 logic: PASS.
7. Material policy is changed in memo but ERP configuration is not updated → implementation incomplete/control failure: PASS.

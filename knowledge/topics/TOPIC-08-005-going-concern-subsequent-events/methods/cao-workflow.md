# CAO Workflow — Going Concern & Subsequent Events

## A. Intake
Resolve framework/version, entity/jurisdiction, reporting date, expected authorization/issuance date, audit status, materiality, financing structure and current liquidity.

Request: board/management forecasts; cash runway; debt/covenants; lender correspondence; refinancing/waiver documents; post-period management accounts; major customer/supplier developments; litigation/regulatory developments; capital raises; restructurings; insurance events; asset sales; insolvency indicators; subsequent-event register; board minutes.

## B. Going-concern assessment
1. Build conditions/events inventory at reporting date and through authorization.
2. Quantify base-case liquidity and covenant headroom over required assessment horizon.
3. Identify dependencies on uncommitted financing, covenant waivers, shareholder support, forecast growth/cost actions and asset sales.
4. Challenge feasibility, timing and control of management mitigating plans.
5. Run downside/sensitivity scenarios around material assumptions.
6. Apply framework-specific threshold: basis appropriateness plus IFRS/AASB/UK material-uncertainty model or US ASC 205-40 substantial-doubt model.
7. Determine disclosure tier: no specific issue; significant judgment; material uncertainty/substantial doubt; non-going-concern basis.
8. Draft conclusion and evidence trail.

## C. Subsequent-event assessment
For every event through cut-off:
- event date and discovery date;
- underlying condition and when it arose;
- evidence available at reporting date;
- affected accounts/disclosures;
- adjusting/recognized conclusion;
- measurement impact;
- non-adjusting/nonrecognized disclosure materiality;
- going-concern interaction;
- owner/reviewer/evidence.

## D. High-risk patterns
- Post-period customer bankruptcy: determine whether it confirms period-end credit deterioration versus new event.
- Covenant breach/waiver/refinancing: separate period-end liability classification from later disclosure and going-concern effects.
- Fire/flood/cyber incident after period end: usually new condition unless evidence points to period-end condition; still assess material disclosure and going concern.
- Litigation settlement: determine whether settlement provides evidence about period-end obligation/estimate.
- Capital raise after period end: generally later financing event, but can be critical evidence in going-concern assessment/disclosure.
- Acquisition/disposal announced after period end: normally non-adjusting, with material disclosure and possibly held-for-sale/commitment facts requiring precise timing analysis.

## E. Outputs
Going-concern paper; liquidity/covenant model; sensitivity pack; subsequent-event register; accounting conclusions; proposed entries; disclosure memo; board/audit-committee summary; PBC/evidence pack; Company Accounting Memory candidates.

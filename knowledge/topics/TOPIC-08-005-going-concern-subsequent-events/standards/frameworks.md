# Framework Knowledge — Going Concern & Subsequent Events

Checked: 2026-09-22
Status: REVIEWED

## IFRS
Primary: IAS 10 Events after the Reporting Period; going-concern presentation requirements currently associated with IAS 1, with effective-period routing to IFRS 18/IAS 8 changes from 2027.

IAS 10 defines the event window from reporting date to authorization for issue and separates events that evidence conditions existing at reporting date from events indicating conditions arising later. Adjust amounts for the former; do not adjust for the latter, but disclose material non-adjusting events. Update disclosures when later information improves evidence about a reporting-date condition. IAS 10 also overrides ordinary event classification when post-period facts show the going-concern basis is no longer appropriate: the basis of preparation changes fundamentally.

CAO routing must capture who authorizes the statements and the authorization date. Late shareholder approval does not automatically extend the event window where statements were already authorized/issued under the entity's governance process.

IFRS Foundation supporting material includes the June 2021 agenda decision on preparation when an entity is no longer a going concern. Do not assume liquidation-basis mechanics are comprehensively prescribed by IFRS merely because going concern is inappropriate; identify the basis selected and supporting policy analysis.

## US GAAP
Primary: ASC 205-40 Presentation of Financial Statements—Going Concern and ASC 855 Subsequent Events.

US GAAP uses a distinct going-concern model centered on management evaluating whether conditions/events raise substantial doubt about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern, management plans and required disclosures. The CAO must not substitute IFRS 'material uncertainty' wording/tests for ASC 205-40.

ASC 855 distinguishes recognized subsequent events (additional evidence about conditions existing at balance-sheet date) from nonrecognized subsequent events (conditions arising after balance-sheet date). Recognized events affect statements; material nonrecognized events generally require disclosure of nature and estimated financial effect or inability to estimate. The evaluation cut-off depends on entity type and issuance/availability-to-be-issued concepts; SEC filers/public-company facts require jurisdiction overlay.

Blocker note: current public FASB search material does not expose the complete Codification paragraph body. Detailed paragraph-level ASC 205-40/855 records remain REFERENCE_ONLY and require direct Codification verification before APPROVED status. This does not block CAO routing/practice build.

## UK GAAP — FRS 102
Primary: FRS 102 Section 3 Financial Statement Presentation and Section 32 Events after the End of the Reporting Period, plus FRC going-concern guidance.

Section 32 follows the adjusting/non-adjusting event architecture. UK-specific paragraphs 32.7A–32.7B address changes in going-concern status, and UK company-law alignment changes some disclosure details compared with IFRS-for-SMEs lineage. The CAO must combine FRS 102 with applicable Companies Act/entity-regime requirements rather than assuming IFRS identity.

The Periodic Review 2024 amendments are generally effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2026. FRC also issued Factsheet 13 in September 2025 for small companies/micro-entities. February 2026 amendments include presentation changes effective 2027 and minor clarifications to Periodic Review 2024, so reporting period/version is mandatory.

## AASB
Primary: AASB 110 Events after the Reporting Period and AASB 101 Presentation of Financial Statements for 2026 periods.

AASB 110 mirrors the IAS 10 core architecture: events between period end and authorization; adjusting evidence of period-end conditions; non-adjusting later conditions; material non-adjusting disclosure; authorization-date disclosure; and a required change away from going concern if post-period facts make that basis inappropriate. AASB 110 paragraphs 14–16 explicitly connect post-period going-concern facts to AASB 101 disclosure requirements.

For Australian conclusions, independently check entity type, reporting tier and Corporations Act/regulatory requirements. Do not treat IFRS reasoning alone as sufficient evidence of AASB compliance.

## Temporal watch
IFRS 18 is effective for annual periods beginning on/after 1 January 2027 and replaces IAS 1, with some IAS 1 material moved to IAS 8/other standards. UK presentation amendments effective 2027 also require version routing. Current 2026 cases should not be silently answered using future-period presentation architecture.

# Authoritative Map — Subsequent Events

Status: REVIEWED
Source checked: 2026-09-22

This record is independently authored. It stores pointers and decision logic, not standards-body text.

## IFRS — IAS 10
Primary source: IFRS Foundation IAS 10 official standard page and supporting-material page.

Core map:
- IAS 10.3: evaluation window and adjusting/non-adjusting categories.
- IAS 10.8–9: adjusting events update recognised amounts / may cause recognition.
- IAS 10.10–11: non-adjusting events do not change period-end recognised amounts.
- IAS 10.12–13: dividends declared after reporting period are not a reporting-date liability.
- IAS 10.14–16: going-concern interaction can change basis of preparation.
- IAS 10.17–18: authorisation-for-issue disclosure.
- IAS 10.19–20: later information about reporting-date conditions updates related disclosures.
- IAS 10.21–22: material non-adjusting events require nature and financial-effect disclosure, or inability to estimate.

CAO interpretation: classify the underlying condition, not merely the calendar date of the confirming evidence. Later information can refine a reporting-date estimate without making every later outcome an adjusting event.

## UK GAAP — FRS 102 Section 32
Primary source: FRC FRS 102 September 2024 edition. Principal Periodic Review 2024 effective date: periods beginning on/after 1 January 2026, with early application permitted.

Core map:
- 32.1–3: scope, definition and evaluation window.
- 32.4–5: adjust recognised amounts and related disclosures for adjusting events; examples include litigation settlement confirming a reporting-date obligation, post-period customer bankruptcy evidencing receivable loss, and post-period inventory sale evidencing reporting-date selling price.
- Section 32 separately addresses non-adjusting events and disclosures.
- 32.7A–7B provide UK-specific going-concern guidance noted by the FRC as a difference from IFRS for SMEs.
- UK company-law differences affect authorisation/disclosure mechanics; do not mechanically inherit IAS 10 wording.

## AASB — AASB 110
Primary source: AASB 110 compilation operative for periods beginning on/after 1 July 2021 and before 1 January 2027.

Core map:
- 3: definition/evaluation window and adjusting/non-adjusting categories.
- 7: window includes events through authorisation even after public profit information.
- 8–9: adjusting events change recognised amounts / may create recognition.
- 10–11: non-adjusting events do not change reporting-date amounts.
- 12–13: post-period dividends are not reporting-date liabilities.
- 17–18: disclose authorisation date and authoriser; disclose amendment power where applicable.
- 19 onward: update disclosures for reporting-date conditions; material non-adjusting events drive disclosure.

Australian routing: current AASB page states Tier 1 for-profit compliance with AASB 110 also achieves IAS 10 compliance; NFP Aus paragraphs and Tier 2 disclosure architecture still require entity/tier context.

## US GAAP — ASC 855
Primary authority: FASB ASC 855. Public FASB ASU material confirms Topic 855 architecture; Codification access remains reference-only.

Core CAO model:
- Recognized subsequent events provide additional evidence about conditions existing at balance-sheet date and affect financial-statement recognition/measurement.
- Nonrecognized subsequent events relate to conditions arising after balance-sheet date; do not revise period-end amounts, but material events may require nature and estimated financial-effect disclosure (or statement that estimate cannot be made).
- Evaluation cut-off differs by entity/filing status: determine whether the applicable model uses issuance or availability-to-be-issued and document the exact date.
- ASC 205-40 is separately invoked for going-concern substantial-doubt analysis.

## Differences that matter operationally
The broad evidence-versus-new-condition concept is similar across frameworks, but terminology, evaluation cut-off, going-concern mechanics and some disclosure/authorisation details differ. Company Context therefore must store framework, entity type, filer/public status, reporting date and authorisation/issuance date before the CAO closes the event log.

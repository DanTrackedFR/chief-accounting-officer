# TOPIC-05-009 — Severance & Termination Benefit Accounting / Vacation & Leave Liability Accounting

Status: **REVIEWED / production-candidate**  
Primary capabilities: CAO-05-017, CAO-05-018  
Sensitivity: H

## CAO objective
Determine when termination/severance and paid-leave obligations exist, distinguish termination consideration from stay/service benefits, measure the liability, produce entries/rollforwards/disclosures, and build an auditable payroll-to-GL process.

## Required facts
Framework/reporting period/entity; employee population; jurisdiction/CBA/employment terms; leave policy and carry-forward/vesting/expiry rules; balances and pay rates; employer on-costs where applicable; termination plan, approvals and communication; affected population; voluntary/involuntary nature; withdrawal rights; service requirements; acceptance windows; payment timing; restructuring linkage; historical leave usage/forfeiture; payroll/HRIS extracts.

## Core decision tree
1. Determine whether payment is for past/future employee service or for termination itself. A payment conditional on future service is not automatically a termination benefit merely because it is in a restructuring package.
2. For termination benefits, establish the point at which the entity cannot withdraw the offer/plan under the applicable framework and whether a restructuring recognition event creates an earlier trigger.
3. Separate base termination amount from retention/stay components and account for each under its correct employee-benefit category.
4. For leave, classify accumulating versus non-accumulating; vesting versus non-vesting; short-term versus other long-term as required.
5. Recognize accumulating paid-absence obligation as service increases future entitlement; recognize non-accumulating absence cost when absence occurs where applicable.
6. Measure using expected settlement, including expected forfeiture/usage and pay-rate assumptions as required; apply discounting/long-term-benefit mechanics where the framework requires.
7. Reconcile HRIS entitlement units × accounting measurement → payroll → GL; test leavers/new hires/manual adjustments and negative balances.
8. Document judgment, calculation, JE, disclosure and evidence.

## IFRS — IAS 19
IAS 19 covers employee benefits other than IFRS 2 share-based payments. Short-term benefits are recognized as service is rendered and measured undiscounted. For paid absences, accumulating entitlements are recognized as service increases future entitlement; non-accumulating absences are recognized when they occur. Termination benefits are recognized at the earlier of when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer and when it recognizes an IAS 37 restructuring cost involving termination benefits. Distinguish termination benefits from benefits in exchange for service. Primary authoritative route: IAS 19, including short-term paid absences and termination-benefit sections. Public repo stores independently authored explanation and pointers, not standard text.

## US GAAP
US GAAP uses different literature depending on arrangement and event, including compensation/employee-benefit and exit/disposal guidance. Do not map the IAS 19 recognition trigger mechanically into US GAAP. Paid leave/compensated absences require current Codification routing based on whether the obligation is attributable to services already rendered, rights vest/accumulate and payment is probable/estimable under applicable guidance. Severance may follow different models for one-time involuntary termination benefits, contractual/ongoing plans, or special arrangements. Final paragraph-level conclusions require current Codification access; public FASB sources are retained as architecture/effective-date evidence only.

## UK GAAP
FRS 102 Section 28 covers employee benefits and explicitly excludes share-based payments to Section 26. It distinguishes short-term, post-employment, other long-term and termination benefits and applies a general recognition principle for employee benefits earned through service. Use the September 2024 edition plus subsequent amendments and reporting-period routing; principal Periodic Review 2024 effective date is 1 January 2026. Confirm current Section 28 text for the entity's period rather than importing IAS 19 by analogy.

## AASB
AASB 119 is the Australian IAS 19-based route. The AASB text confirms accumulating paid absences are recognized when service increases entitlement and non-accumulating absences when they occur. It also illustrates separating termination consideration from incremental benefits earned through continued service. Independently check the period-applicable AASB 119 compilation and entity-specific disclosure tier.

## Calculation methods
### Leave liability
For each employee/cohort: eligible entitlement units × expected settlement pay rate × expected utilization/vesting factor, plus directly attributable employer on-costs where the applicable accounting basis requires. Separate current/long-term classification and discount where required. Reconcile opening liability + service earned + rate/on-cost/assumption effects − leave taken − cash paid/forfeited ± transfers = closing liability.

### Termination plan
Build employee-level or cohort schedule: eligible headcount × contractual/plan amount, split termination component from future-service component, probability/acceptance mechanics where applicable, payment timing/discounting where applicable, payments and reversals. Tie plan population to approved HR/legal source.

## Journal-entry outputs
Employee-benefit/severance expense (or eligible asset capitalization only where another standard requires), termination/leave liability, payroll payable/cash, and true-up/reversal entries. Expense classification follows nature/function presentation policy and must reconcile to compensation reporting.

## Controls / audit / systems
- HR/legal approval and communication trigger to accounting;
- completeness of affected employee population;
- approved leave policy master and jurisdiction mapping;
- HRIS-to-payroll-to-GL reconciliation;
- independent review of rates, on-costs, utilization/forfeiture and discount assumptions;
- leaver and manual-adjustment exception reports;
- restructuring memo cross-reference;
- disclosure and current/non-current tie-out.

## Practice classification
**Required:** correct benefit category, recognition trigger, measurement, entries and disclosures.  
**Recommended:** monthly leave rollforward and event-driven termination accounting with HR/legal certification.  
**World-class:** controlled HRIS/payroll/ERP lineage, cohort analytics, automated exception testing and assumption back-testing.  
**Shortcut/risk:** expensing all severance on management intent alone; treating stay bonuses as termination benefits; booking gross HRIS leave hours without accounting measurement.

## TrackedFR applicability
Relevant where recurring HRIS/payroll/ERP extracts require reconciliation, employee/cohort matching and exception analysis. Not a substitute for legal interpretation of employee entitlements.

## Scenario tests
1. Announced closure pays every employee base severance plus extra amount only if they remain through closure → split termination and service components.
2. Accumulating annual leave carried forward and paid on exit → accrue as service increases entitlement; reconcile units/rates.
3. Non-accumulating sick leave → do not build an accumulating liability merely from unused days.
4. Voluntary redundancy offer can still be withdrawn at reporting date → test framework-specific recognition trigger.
5. Material long-service/long-term leave → route out of simplistic short-term model and assess long-term measurement.

Expected: classification, timing, measurement, JE, evidence, controls and framework-specific caveats produced for each.

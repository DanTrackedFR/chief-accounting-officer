# Dependency-based reporting calendar and certification case

**Critical path.** Suppose approval is due day 30 after year-end. Consolidation TB day 8 precedes tax provision day 12, draft accounts day 16, audit review day 22 and board approval day 30. Valuation for a material acquisition is due day 10 and feeds tax, PPA, goodwill, segment/related-party and disclosure work. If the valuation slips to day 18, tax and draft cannot be certified on their original evidence version. Project owner records valuation-deliverable dependency, accountable specialist, missing input, affected assertions, earliest feasible completion and escalation to CAO/board secretary. Simply changing task status to green while using provisional, unapproved valuation is a control failure. A genuine provisional PPA is separately justified under applicable acquisition guidance and disclosed where required.

**Certification design.** Every certificate includes scope (entities/period/assertions), evidence IDs, source version, exceptions, preparer, reviewer, timestamp and downstream recipients. Entity controllers certify TB completeness and reconciliations; treasury certifies debt/covenant/cash events; legal and tax provide their owned evidence; technical accounting owns difficult recognition/measurement conclusions; reporting certifies consolidated tie-out and disclosure coverage. The CAO reviews unresolved material issues before authorization. Build a late-change propagation table: late debt amendment → 06-006, current/noncurrent classification, going concern, subsequent events, cash-flow financing, covenants note and final tie-out; late share issue → equity, EPS and cash flow. Reopen only affected certifications with documented impact, and rerun downstream checks.

**Metrics that diagnose.** On-time completion alone masks quality. Record first-pass accepted/rejected deliverables, aged material review points, late top-side entries, recurring manual bridge exceptions and subsequent audit adjustments. Investigate root cause and process/data fix, retaining unaltered historical metrics. A zero-exception declaration without a supporting evidence index is not a PASS. If approval deadline is statutory, verify jurisdiction-specific law and actual dates; accounting standards do not prescribe one universal filing calendar. IFRS 18 annual periods from 2027 and FRS 102 Periodic Review 2024 principally from 2026 are implementation gates that enter the calendar *before* first reporting date, with transition/reclassification tasks and comparative data.

**Executed challenge.** Day-18 valuation against day-16 draft: fail unchanged day-16 disclosure certificate. Late debt journal after day-22 audit review: reopen downstream classification, forecast and tie-out. Day-30 board approval without a signed material legal contingencies schedule: escalate and do not infer nil claims. Release evidence: baseline calendar, dependency graph, exception decisions, signed source-version certificates, quality review, board authorization and filed version. This topic is operational; authoritative references attach to the underlying accounting topic and the entity's filing jurisdiction rather than pretending that a cross-framework calendar standard exists.

# Compensation exception: reconcile cost, liabilities and asset capitalization

The retained payroll-to-GL bridge yields 1,000 gross register +60 bonus +20 leave −10 qualifying capitalization =1,070 wages, but GL wages 1,085; a 15 exception remains. Investigate GL 15 manual journal with source timesheets. If it is December employer contribution, it belongs to benefit expense/payable, not gross wage line; reclassify only after confirming plan type, period and accrual. If it is an unmatched payroll interface duplicate, reverse with system/bank evidence; if legitimate earned wages missing from payroll, adjust accrual and register reconciliation. Each path changes disclosures and liabilities differently despite closing the 15 numerical difference. A reviewer must record root cause and source IDs, not approve a generic “payroll accrual 15.”

**Capitalization fork.** The −10 exclusion assumes time records and a specific qualifying asset/contract-cost standard support capitalization. If the 10 is routine administration or pre-contract speculative sales time, return to expense and expected GL wages would be 1,080 before resolving other items. Do not capitalize from cost-center label. Share awards require separate IFRS 2/ASC 718/FRS 102 Section 26/AASB 2 schedule, and defined-benefit obligation is not payroll gross plus an arbitrary actuarial percentage. Tie wage expense, payroll liabilities, cash remittance, employer tax, capitalized labour and benefit/share-award notes independently.

**Data/control.** Join employee ID, entity, pay code, service period, GL account, payroll run/version, journal ID and legal employment status. Reconcile starters/leavers, retro-pay, reversals, intercompany recharge and FX; restrict manual payroll JEs and run post-close backtest. Fail a balanced 1,085 bridge achieved by posting 15 to “other,” or capitalization of 10 without work orders. This is operational practice; specific compensation and asset recognition routes to TOPIC-05-005–009 and the asset/contract topic. Rights/period-specific FRS 102 and AASB/ASC citations reside there; no invented universal reconciliation standard.

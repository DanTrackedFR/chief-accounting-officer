# TOPIC-05-009 — Severance & Termination Benefits / Vacation & Leave Liabilities

Status: **REVIEWED / production-candidate**
Primary capabilities: CAO-05-017, CAO-05-018
Sensitivity: H
Frameworks: IFRS, US GAAP, UK GAAP, AASB
Source check: 2026-09-23

## Objective
Enable the CAO to distinguish benefits earned through service from benefits paid in exchange for termination, determine the recognition trigger, measure leave obligations, prepare the accounting and evidence, and route restructuring/SBC/pension issues to adjacent topics.

## Required facts
Obtain entity/framework/reporting period; employment and award terms; whether termination is voluntary/involuntary; communication/approval dates; withdrawal rights; required future service; affected population; statutory/contractual/constructive rights; leave type and carry-forward/vesting rules; payroll rates/on-costs; expected utilisation/forfeiture; settlement timing; restructuring linkage; materiality.

## Principles
1. Classify the economic substance before measuring. A payment conditional on future service is not automatically a termination benefit merely because it is connected to a restructuring.
2. Termination accounting focuses on the event that creates the obligation and whether an offer can still be withdrawn. Service benefits accrue as employees render service.
3. Accumulating leave can create an obligation before leave is taken. Non-accumulating leave is generally recognised when the absence occurs.
4. Separate termination, retention/stay, bonus, pension, share-based and restructuring components when a package contains more than one economic promise.
5. Measurement must reconcile employee-level source data to payroll/HR records and the GL; assumptions about utilisation, forfeiture, pay rates, on-costs and settlement timing require evidence.

## Framework routing
### IFRS — IAS 19
IAS 19 covers employee benefits other than share-based payments. Termination benefits are recognised at the earlier of when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer and when it recognises IAS 37 restructuring costs involving termination benefits. Short-term employee benefits are recognised as service is rendered and measured at the undiscounted amount expected to be paid. For paid absences, determine whether rights accumulate and whether settlement is expected within the short-term classification window. Use current IAS 19 and IAS 37 sources for production conclusions.

### AASB — AASB 119
AASB 119 follows the IAS 19 architecture. Current official text explicitly requires accumulating short-term paid absences to be recognised as service increases future entitlement and measured as the additional amount expected to be paid from unused entitlement; non-accumulating absences are recognised when they occur. Termination benefits are separately classified because the obligation arises from termination rather than service. Australian statutory leave arrangements and entity-specific industrial instruments remain facts, not accounting-standard assumptions.

### UK GAAP — FRS 102 Section 28
Route employee-benefit and termination conclusions through the current FRS 102 Section 28 edition applicable to the reporting period. The Periodic Review 2024 amendments have a principal effective date of 1 January 2026. Do not infer UK requirements solely from IAS 19; confirm current Section 28 and effective-date requirements. Current public FRC standard is reference-only in this repository.

### US GAAP
Route compensated absences and termination arrangements through the applicable current Codification topics (including ASC 710/712 and related restructuring guidance as applicable). US GAAP has detailed plan-type and communication/acceptance recognition requirements; do not substitute the IAS 19 trigger. Because public FASB paths do not expose all current Codification paragraph bodies, paragraph-level conclusions remain PARTIAL unless independently verified against authorised current Codification access.

## CAO decision workflow
1. Establish framework, period, jurisdiction and employment-law facts.
2. Inventory each promised payment/benefit separately.
3. Ask what earns each component: past/current service, future service, or termination/acceptance of termination.
4. Determine whether the obligation is legal, contractual or constructive and identify the recognition event.
5. For leave, classify accumulating/non-accumulating, vesting/non-vesting, short/long-term and cash-settleable/use-only features.
6. Build employee-level population and reconcile opening entitlement + earned + adjustments - used/paid = closing entitlement.
7. Apply pay-rate, on-cost, utilisation/forfeiture and discount assumptions required by the framework and benefit horizon.
8. Separate restructuring provision from employee-benefit liability where required; avoid double counting.
9. Prepare JE, rollforward, current/non-current presentation and disclosures.
10. Produce conclusion paper and promote durable policy/election/methodology facts to Company Accounting Memory.

## Calculation patterns
Leave rollforward: closing units = opening units + units earned + approved adjustments - units taken/paid/forfeited. Liability is the framework-appropriate expected settlement amount for the recognised entitlement, including directly attributable employer costs where required by policy/law. Termination population model: eligible employees × contractual/statutory offer, adjusted only for framework-supported probability/acceptance/service conditions. Never use a headcount-times-average shortcut when employee terms or eligibility are heterogeneous and material.

## Accounting outputs
Typical entries include Dr employee compensation expense / Cr leave or termination liability; settlement Dr liability / Cr cash/payroll payable. Capitalisation to an asset is only permitted when another applicable standard permits/requires employee costs in asset cost. Classification and disclosure depend on framework, settlement timing and materiality.

## Documentation and controls
Minimum evidence: approved plan/communications; employment/collective agreements; HR/payroll entitlement extract; employee population completeness reconciliation; recognition-date memo; service-v-termination component analysis; rate/on-cost assumptions; calculation and rollforward; JE support; reviewer evidence; disclosure tie-out.

Controls: HR/legal-to-accounting trigger for restructurings and terminations; approved leave rules; payroll-to-GL reconciliation; employee-population completeness; opening-to-closing entitlement rollforward; independent review of recognition trigger and material assumptions; stale/manual adjustment review; disclosure tie-out; change control over payroll/leave configuration.

## Systems / automation
Use HRIS/payroll as entitlement evidence but validate accounting mapping and completeness. A recurring reconciliation across HRIS/payroll, restructuring trackers, ERP/GL and Excel is a genuine TrackedFR candidate because it is multi-system, recurring and reconciliation-heavy; a one-off termination memo is not.

## Audit challenge points
Expect challenge on recognition timing, future-service conditions disguised as termination benefits, completeness of affected employees, leave utilisation/forfeiture assumptions, statutory/contractual terms, restructuring double counting, current/non-current classification and post-period plan changes.

## Framework differences that must not be flattened
- IFRS/AASB termination recognition architecture must not be applied mechanically to US GAAP.
- UK GAAP requires direct current FRS 102 verification, especially across effective periods.
- Jurisdictional employment law determines enforceable rights and may change the fact pattern without changing the accounting framework.
- Accumulating versus non-accumulating leave is a substantive recognition distinction.

## Scenario tests
1. Closure announced with unconditional severance plus ten-month retention premium: split termination and service components.
2. Voluntary offer still withdrawable: do not assume IFRS recognition until the applicable trigger is met.
3. Accumulating annual leave carried forward: accrue expected recognised obligation.
4. Non-accumulating jury leave: recognise when absence occurs, not a year-end bank.
5. Non-vesting accumulating sick leave: assess expected additional cost rather than assuming zero because no cash payout exists.
6. US entity with one-time involuntary plan: route to current US plan-specific guidance, not IAS 19.
7. UK period beginning in 2026: use current FRS 102 effective-period routing.
8. Australian entity with material annual/long-service leave: use AASB 119 plus applicable legal/industrial facts.
9. Restructuring provision includes severance already accrued in employee benefits: identify and remove double count.
10. Recurring global leave reconciliation across payroll systems and ERP: design controlled data reconciliation and assess TrackedFR applicability.

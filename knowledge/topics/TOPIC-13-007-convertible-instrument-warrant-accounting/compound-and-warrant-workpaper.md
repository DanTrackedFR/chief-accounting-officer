# Convertible debt and warrant classification case

**Issuer facts.** Plain non-derivative note issues for 1,000, bears cash coupon and converts at holder option into a fixed number of the issuer's ordinary shares for a fixed principal amount. Assume IFRS classification finds a liability plus an equity conversion option under IAS 32 and a comparable liability without conversion has fair value 920. Initial gross split is liability 920 and equity 80; there is no day-one gain. Incremental directly attributable issue costs are 20; allocate proportionally on this simplified gross-value basis: liability 18.4 and equity 1.6, yielding initial liability carrying amount 901.6 and net equity component 78.4. Cash 1,000−20=980 agrees to 901.6+78.4=980. Build a liability effective-interest schedule using contractual coupons and maturity, with the 901.6 opening carrying amount, and reconcile annual interest and cash to the debt register. These numbers are illustrative; derive yield and fair value from dated market evidence.

**Warrant fork.** A detachable warrant has a variable exercise price indexed to an unrelated share price and gives the issuer a potentially variable amount of cash for a fixed number of own shares. The simple fixed-for-fixed assumption no longer holds; analyze the complete IAS 32 terms and exceptions and, if classified as derivative liability, initially recognize at fair value and remeasure under IFRS 9. If initial fair value is 25 and closing fair value 31, the illustrative change is a 6 loss before other adjustments. Do not treat the warrant as part of the 80 residual if it is a separately allocated freestanding instrument; a combined transaction requires a new proceeds/allocation analysis, not simple addition of cases. A warrant with a foreign-currency strike requires the applicable exception analysis. A price becoming fixed without contract amendment does not itself establish a universal reclassification rule.

**Framework and release.** IFRS IAS 32 paragraphs 28–32, IFRS 9 and IFRS 7; AASB 132/9/7 versions; FRS 102 Section 22 and Sections 11/12 for relevant terms. US ASC 470/480/815/260 analysis differs materially; do not reuse 920/80 separation without current Codification review. FRS 102 Periodic Review 2024 chiefly applies to periods beginning 1 January 2026. The reviewer signs a clause-by-clause settlement matrix, valuation, cost invoices, debt schedule, derivative sensitivity, legal cap-table-to-GL reconciliation and EPS/disclosure handoff. Negative tests: variable shares posted as plain equity; day-one 80 booked as income; cost allocation 20 entirely against debt; unverified US IFRS-style split all fail.

# TOPIC-05-003 — Vendor Credits/Debit Balances; Provisions & Contingencies

Status: REVIEWED / production-candidate
Built: 2026-09-23
Capabilities: CAO-05-005, CAO-05-006

## Sources and framework routing
Primary official source families: IAS 37; FASB ASC 450 plus topic-specific guidance; FRS 102 Section 21; AASB 137. Source-body text is not reproduced. AASB 137 period/version must be resolved; AASB published a January 2026 compilation for specified 2025/26 periods. FRS 102 Periodic Review 2024 changes include Section 21 scope/disclosure amendments.

## PRINCIPLES
Separate ordinary trade accruals/payables from provisions: provisions have greater uncertainty in timing/amount. For every uncertain obligation determine: past event; present legal/constructive obligation; probability threshold under applicable framework; measurability; best estimate/range; discounting where applicable; reimbursement; reassessment; presentation/disclosure. Never use a provision as a smoothing reserve.

Vendor debit balances/credits require substance analysis: unapplied vendor credit, prepayment/receivable, refund due, disputed item, duplicate payment, contra-payable or classification error. Aging alone does not determine accounting.

## IFRS — IAS 37
Recognize a provision when a present legal/constructive obligation from a past event exists, outflow is probable and a reliable estimate can be made. Measure the rational settlement/transfer amount, considering risks/uncertainties and present value when material. Future operating losses are not provisions. Contingent liabilities are not recognized but generally disclosed unless outflow is remote. Contingent assets are not recognized; disclose when inflow is probable and recognize only once no longer contingent/virtually certain. Onerous contracts route to TOPIC-05-004. Reimbursements are separate assets only at the required high certainty threshold.

## US GAAP
ASC 450-20 provides the loss-contingency framework: accrue when loss is probable and reasonably estimable; disclosure can be required for unaccrued reasonably possible loss/exposure. US probability terminology and range measurement can differ from IFRS; do not import IAS 37's 'best estimate' mechanics automatically. Topic-specific guidance overrides general contingency guidance where applicable. Public FASB staff material confirms general business risks are not loss-contingency accruals.

## UK GAAP
FRS 102 Section 21 recognizes a provision for an obligation from a past event when transfer of economic benefits is probable and amount can be measured reliably. Section 21.6 is central to present-obligation analysis; 21.4 recognition; 21.7 measurement; 21.12 contingent-liability disclosure; 21.14 onward disclosures. Periodic Review 2024 expanded Section 21 scope for financial guarantee contracts subject to specified elections and changed contingent-asset/company-law disclosure details.

## AASB
AASB 137 follows the IAS 37 core for for-profit Tier 1 entities but Australian paragraphs and reporting tier/entity type must be checked. Current-period compilation is mandatory. AASB includes public-sector-specific guidance, including government policy/promise fact patterns; do not inherit IFRS blindly for Australian public-sector/NFP cases.

## DIFFERENCES
IFRS/AASB and US GAAP differ importantly in probability language and measurement of ranges; a cross-framework memo must state the actual consequence. FRS 102 is similar in principle but not identical in scope/disclosure. Business-combination contingencies route to acquisition guidance rather than mechanically applying the ordinary model.

## CAO workflow
1 resolve framework/period/entity; 2 identify obligation and obligating event; 3 distinguish payable/accrual/provision/contingency; 4 obtain legal/operational evidence; 5 assess probability using framework-specific threshold; 6 build estimate/range and discount analysis; 7 assess reimbursements; 8 determine JE/classification; 9 disclosures; 10 reassess each close; 11 preserve judgment/evidence.

## Documentation / controls / audit
Provision register with owner, legal basis, event date, probability, estimate method, range, rate, expected settlement, reimbursement, movements, source evidence and review. Controls over completeness from legal claims, contracts, complaints, warranties, environmental matters and board papers; quarterly legal-letter interface; rollforward; stale-item review; disclosure tie-out.

## Scenarios
Litigation with uncertain outcome: route probability/estimate by framework, not one global threshold. Vendor has net debit from duplicate payment: investigate recoverability and classify based on substance. Management wants restructuring reserve before constructive obligation: reject premature recognition. Climate regulation creates remediation obligation: establish obligating event and measurement rather than reserve for general future business risk.

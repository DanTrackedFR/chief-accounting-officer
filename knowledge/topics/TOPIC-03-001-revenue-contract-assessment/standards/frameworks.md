# Framework Knowledge — Revenue Contract Assessment

Checked: 2026-09-22
Status: REVIEWED

## IFRS 15
IFRS 15 is effective from 1 January 2018. Its core principle is revenue reflecting transfer of promised goods/services for expected consideration, implemented through the five-step model. For this topic, the CAO first confirms the arrangement is a contract with a customer in IFRS 15 scope, then identifies performance obligations/promises. Scope must be separated from leases, insurance, financial instruments and other specifically scoped literature where applicable.

Contract assessment requires enforceable rights/obligations, commercial substance and the framework's contract criteria, including collectibility assessment. Contract combination and modification rules prevent artificial contract splitting. Promises can be explicit or arise from customary business practices/published policies/specific statements. A promised good/service is separately accounted for when distinct under the standard's two-part distinct analysis; otherwise combine until a distinct bundle is identified. Series guidance can aggregate qualifying repeated distinct services into one performance obligation.

The 2024 IASB PIR concluded IFRS 15 is working as intended, while flagging principal-agent, consideration payable to customers and interactions with other standards for possible future consideration. These are update-monitoring items, not permission to change current accounting.

## US GAAP — ASC 606
Topic 606 was developed jointly with IFRS 15 and shares the core five-step revenue model. Scope applies to contracts with customers except arrangements governed by other Codification topics such as leases/insurance/financial instruments. The CAO must resolve US-specific scope, collectibility and enforceability using current Codification and applicable legal context.

FASB's 2024 PIR concluded the revenue standard is broadly achieving its objective. Post-issuance amendments continue: for example, 2025 guidance addressed share-based consideration payable to a customer. Therefore source/effective-date monitoring remains mandatory.

Public FASB materials confirm Topic 606's architecture, but full current Codification paragraph-by-paragraph approval remains REFERENCE_ONLY where the public route does not expose the complete text.

## UK GAAP — revised FRS 102 Section 23
The Periodic Review 2024 rewrote Section 23 Revenue from Contracts with Customers, generally effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2026. FRC Factsheet 10 confirms a single comprehensive five-step model.

Verified official FRC mapping:
- 23.4: five-step model.
- 23.5–23.6: consistent application / portfolio approach when results would not differ materially.
- 23.7: contract criteria before revenue-model accounting.
- 23.8: reassess only when significant facts/circumstances change after inception criteria are met.
- 23.9–23.10: consideration received before contract criteria are met remains a liability until criteria/revenue conditions are satisfied.
- 23.12: combine contracts entered at/near same time with same/related customer when package/single-objective, cross-dependent pricing/performance, or single-performance-obligation conditions apply.
- 23.17–23.25: performance obligations and distinct assessment.
- 23.26–23.35: warranties, upfront fees and customer options/material rights.

FRC explicitly notes revised Section 23 is based on IFRS 15 principles but includes simplifications and policy choices; it is designed to allow, not require, some policies that would meet IFRS 15. Do not treat it as an IFRS clone.

## AASB 15
AASB 15 incorporates IFRS 15 and retains the five-step model. The current AASB material maps contract identification to paragraphs 9–21, performance obligations to 22–30, transaction price to 46–72, allocation to 73–86 and satisfaction to 31–45. AASB includes Australian not-for-profit application material; entity type is therefore required context before blindly inheriting a for-profit IFRS conclusion.

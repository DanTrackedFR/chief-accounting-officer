## Contract-to-obligation case

A customer signs one order for platform access for 12 months, a separately usable training day and a customized data conversion. Before model entry, check both parties' approval, identifiable rights/payment terms, commercial substance and collectibility under the applicable standard. Determine whether the conversion significantly modifies or integrates platform access; if so, the conversion and access might be one combined obligation, whereas the training may remain distinct. A stand-alone price list alone does not answer whether promises are distinct *in the context of this contract*. Side letters and pre-sales customary promises enter the population if they create enforceable/valid expectations. If the contract-existence gate fails, do not force the invoice into ordinary revenue recognition; evaluate consideration received under the relevant framework and reassess.

Assume after analysis that the three promises are distinct with SSP 900, 200, 400, respectively, and fixed contract consideration 1,200. Relative allocation is 720, 160, 320 (SSP total 1,500; weights 60%, 13⅓%, 26⅔%). Recognize only when/as each promise transfers under its own timing analysis. A 1,200 invoice paid on day one is not 1,200 day-one revenue. **Executed:** 720+160+320=1,200; PASS arithmetic, distinctness and timing conditional. If conversion/access are one combined obligation, reidentify SSP and allocation; do not reuse these weights.

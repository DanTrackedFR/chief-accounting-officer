# TOPIC-02-005 — Reconciliation Governance & Account Certification

Status: REVIEWED — Phase 2D
Primary capabilities: CAO-02-011, CAO-02-012

## Governance objective
Ensure every in-scope balance-sheet account has an accountable owner, appropriate reconciliation method/frequency, evidence standard, reviewer and escalation path.

## Account inventory
For each account/entity combination maintain: purpose; normal balance; source/subledger; reconciliation method; risk tier; frequency; preparer; reviewer; due date; materiality/tolerance; aging rule; certification requirement; policy exception; automation status.

## Certification
Certification is an explicit assertion that the account has been reconciled under policy and material issues are either resolved or transparently escalated. Certification must not be available merely because a task was marked complete.

## Methods
Source-to-GL; subledger-to-GL; rollforward; proof-of-balance; transaction-level matching; external confirmation/evidence; reasonableness/analytical support only when suitable for the account and risk. The CAO challenges weak methods, especially self-reconciliation to another ledger-derived schedule.

## Exceptions
Missing source; unsupported difference; stale item; late reconciliation; reviewer conflict; unexplained movement; policy deviation. Track severity, owner, target date, accounting/reporting impact and recurrence.

## Metrics
On-time preparation/review; material unreconciled value; aged items; repeat exceptions; policy exceptions; certification completion; auto-match rate; manual adjustments generated from reconciliations.

## Tests
- Account certified with $500k unexplained difference: FAIL regardless of checkbox.
- Low-risk immaterial account using quarterly rollforward under approved policy: potentially acceptable.
- Same person prepares source schedule and reconciliation: assess source/control independence and reviewer precision.
- 100% automated match but source interface incomplete: FAIL completeness.

## Practice classification
Accounting standards generally determine recognition/measurement, not the mechanics of account certification. This is CAO practice/control infrastructure and must be presented as such, not mislabeled as IFRS/GAAP requirement.

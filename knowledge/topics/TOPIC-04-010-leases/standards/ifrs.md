# IFRS — IFRS 16 Leases

Source: SRC-IFRS-IFRS16  
Status: REVIEW CANDIDATE  
Checked: 2026-09-22

## Model
IFRS 16 uses a control-based definition of a lease. A contract contains a lease when, for consideration, it conveys control over use of an identified asset for a period. Analysis therefore tests identified asset, economic benefits and decision-making over use. See IFRS 16.9 and B9–B31.

For lessees, the core model generally recognizes a right-of-use (ROU) asset and lease liability. Recognition exemptions exist for short-term leases and leases of low-value underlying assets. See IFRS 16.5–8.

## Lessee workflow
1. Scope and lease identification — IFRS 16.3–4, 9–11, B9–B33.
2. Separate lease/non-lease components; apply allocation requirements or relevant lessee practical expedient — 12–17.
3. Determine enforceable lease term including option periods when the exercise/non-exercise threshold is met; reassess when required — 18–21, B34–B41.
4. At commencement recognize ROU asset and liability — 22.
5. Initially measure ROU asset from liability plus applicable prepayments/direct costs/restoration-type amounts, net of incentives — 23–25.
6. Measure liability at present value of unpaid lease payments; use rate implicit if readily determinable, otherwise lessee incremental borrowing rate — 26–28.
7. Subsequently accrete interest/reduce liability for payments and remeasure for specified changes — 36–46.
8. ROU asset generally follows cost-model depreciation and impairment mechanics, with specified interactions/elections — 29–35.
9. Present and disclose lease effects under 47–60.

## Payments and rate
Lease-liability measurement includes fixed/in-substance fixed payments, index/rate-dependent variable payments measured using the commencement-date index/rate, specified residual-value guarantee exposure, purchase option price when exercise is reasonably certain, and termination penalties when the lease term reflects termination. Other usage/performance variable payments are generally expensed when the triggering event occurs.

## Modifications
A modification is a separate lease when it adds right(s) of use and consideration is commensurate with standalone price (44). Other modifications generally require remeasurement using a revised discount rate; decreases in scope also adjust the ROU asset and recognize the resulting gain/loss (45–46).

## Lessor
Lessors retain finance-versus-operating classification based on transfer of substantially all risks/rewards (61–66). Finance leases recognize a net investment; operating leases generally retain the underlying asset and recognize lease income over the lease term (67–97).

## Sale and leaseback
Apply the IFRS 15 sale test to determine whether the transfer is a sale before applying IFRS 16 sale-and-leaseback accounting (98–103). The 2022 Lease Liability in a Sale and Leaseback amendment added subsequent measurement requirements and is effective for annual periods beginning on/after 1 Jan 2024.

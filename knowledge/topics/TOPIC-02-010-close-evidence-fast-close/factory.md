# TOPIC-02-010 — Close Evidence & Sign-Off / Fast-Close Assessment & Design

Status: REVIEWED
Capabilities: CAO-02-021, CAO-02-022
Knowledge mix: PRINCIPLES + PRACTICE.

## Objective
Define when close work is actually complete and accelerate the close without degrading accounting evidence, review or control quality.

## Evidence standard
Evidence must show what was performed, population/period/entity, source, preparer, reviewer, date, result, exceptions and resolution. A screenshot can corroborate but is weak if it does not establish population completeness, logic or reproducibility. Prefer governed exports, calculations, system logs and linked source documents.

Evidence should be retained at the level needed to reproduce a material accounting conclusion. Judgment-heavy areas require facts, assumptions, method, alternatives/challenge and conclusion, not only a final number.

## Sign-off hierarchy
Task sign-off → account/process certification → entity close certification → consolidation/reporting certification. Higher-level sign-off relies on lower-level completion plus unresolved-exception visibility; it does not erase exceptions.

Close certification should state: required tasks completed; material accounts reconciled; significant estimates/judgments reviewed; unresolved items listed; post-close entries controlled; TB version identified; reporting impacts assessed.

## Fast-close diagnostic
Measure actual task timestamps and dependency waits for several periods. Split elapsed time into work time, wait-for-data, wait-for-review, rework and system delay. Target the constraint, not the calendar symptom.

Improvement hierarchy:
1. Remove unnecessary duplicate work.
2. Improve upstream data timeliness/quality.
3. Standardize accounting methods and evidence.
4. Shift valid work pre-close with true-up controls.
5. Parallelize independent work.
6. Automate stable data extraction/matching/calculation.
7. Make review exception-led and risk-based.
8. Redesign systems/interfaces where structural bottlenecks remain.

## Unsafe acceleration
Skipping material reconciliations; using unsupported plugs; eliminating independent review; accepting unexplained differences; closing before source completeness; using forecast values without controlled true-up; suppressing late corrections. CAO should explicitly quantify/describe risk if management chooses a shortcut.

## Maturity model
Reactive: heroics, unclear status, evidence scattered. Controlled: calendar/ownership/reconciliations. Standardized: policies/templates/dependencies. Scaled: integrated systems/automation/exception review. World-class: continuous accounting, high source quality, near-real-time reconciliation, auditable lineage and close focused on exceptions/judgments.

## Controls
Evidence completeness dashboard; required sign-offs; unresolved-exception register; final TB hash/version; period lock; certification; evidence retention/access; retrospective close KPI review.

## TrackedFR fit
Useful for accelerating recurring cross-system reconciliations/data preparation that sit on critical path. Not positioned as a close-management/calendar replacement.

## Artifacts
Evidence standard; certification forms; fast-close diagnostic; constraint map; automation backlog; target close calendar; risk register; 30/60/90-day roadmap.

## Scenarios
Eight-day close with three days waiting for AP: diagnose source/process cutoff and uninvoiced liability workflow before pressuring accounting reviewers. Five-day target achieved by skipping recs: classify as unacceptable shortcut unless explicit risk decision and remediation. Automated rec saves one day but exceptions reviewed after reporting: redesign SLA so automation actually shortens controlled close.

## QA
PASS: defines evidence/certification and treats fast close as constraint removal, not control removal.

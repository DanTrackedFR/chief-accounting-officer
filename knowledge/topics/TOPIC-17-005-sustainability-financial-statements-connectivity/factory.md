# Sustainability-to-financial-statements connectivity and climate assumption consistency

Companion to prior README; capabilities CAO-17-010/011. Source checked 2026-09-27. Proposed REVIEWED candidate for comparison and escalation method.

## Accounting versus sustainability objectives

[IFRS S1](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s1-general-requirements/) expects connected sustainability-related financial information; [IFRS S2](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/) addresses climate risks, opportunities and related current/anticipated financial effects. Financial-statement recognition still follows the entity's accounting framework and transaction facts: IAS 36/AASB 136 impairment; ASC 350/360 US asset tests; FRS 102 Sections 18/27 and UK goodwill differences; IAS 37/ASC 450/FRS 102 Section 21/AASB 137 provisions; useful lives, fair value, ECL, tax, going concern and commitments/disclosures. The same climate fact may require sustainability disclosure but not a journal, or may support changed accounting estimates. Do not treat an ESRS double-materiality conclusion as a substitute for financial-statement materiality or a provision recognition test.

## Assumption bridge and challenge

For each material risk/plan build fields: site/product, source and approval date, reporting horizon, physical/transition scenario, probability or range, carbon/energy price, demand, capex, asset closure date, model owner, sustainability output, accounting output, sensitivity and explanation for divergence. Ask whether the risk existed at the financial statement date, is sufficiently supported, affects a recognized balance or disclosure, and how the accounting measurement objective differs. Trace plans to board-approved forecasts and post-period updates. Compare budgets/capital allocation, impairment cash flows, useful lives, fair-value assumptions, decommissioning liabilities and going-concern scenarios to public targets. A target without an approved action may still be decision-useful sustainability information but may not meet the accounting threshold for a liability.

## Worked impairment bridge

A CGU has carrying amount 10.0m and base-case recoverable amount 10.5m, so 0.5m headroom. A separately supportable transition scenario reduces recoverable amount to 9.8m; under that scenario the shortfall would be 0.2m (10.0−9.8), not a booked base-case loss by arithmetic alone. Determine whether the scenario is reasonably possible, whether the model applies the relevant framework's value-in-use or fair-value rules, whether cash-flow and discount-rate assumptions double count risk, and whether sensitivity/headroom disclosure is required. If management publicly commits to close a plant in 2030 but the financial model assumes 2045 use, obtain a reconciled rationale or revise estimate; a one-line assertion that horizons differ is not evidence.

## Controls and executed scenarios

At each financial reporting cycle compare an approved assumption inventory across sustainability report, financial statements, MD&A/narrative and management forecasts. Track contradictions with owner, evidence, quantified exposure, resolution and disclosure location. Review specialists' work and data, and preserve the source/model version. S1: climate transition plan says mandatory closure while impairment base assumes indefinite operation with no explanation → FAIL. S2: carbon price 80/t stress and 45/t base with evidence, probability and headroom sensitivity → defensible difference, PASS conditional on model. S3: climate risk disclosure automatically becomes provision → FAIL until present-obligation and probability/measurement criteria tested. S4: disclosure of 0.5m headroom but signed model shows 0.2m after a late change → fail tie-out. Dependencies 04-002/006, 05-003, 08-005, 15-004 and 17-002. Residual: model-specific standards and actual evidence determine recognition/disclosure, not this illustrative comparison.

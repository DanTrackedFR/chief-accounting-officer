# Bank reconciliation and restriction case

Capabilities CAO-06-001/002/003. The existing README supplies framework routing; this case clarifies the two-sided bank/book calculation. Never label a bank-side timing item as a book-side adjustment.

At 31 December, bank statement balance 900. Outstanding payments issued by the entity but not cleared are 70; a genuine deposit in transit is 40. Adjusted bank balance = 900−70+40=870. GL cash balance before bank-only entries is 890. Bank fee 10 and direct customer receipt 10 are absent from books, net zero; posting both leaves adjusted books 890, still 20 above adjusted bank. Find the remaining 20; equal net bank-only items do not explain it. If a further 20 unrecorded debit is evidenced, corrected GL 870. **Executed:** bank 870; books 890−10+10=890; difference 20 FAIL until evidenced. No balancing plug.

An internal transfer of 100 leaves Bank A on 31 December and reaches Bank B on 2 January. Use one transfer ID and examine settlement rights and group cash cut-off; do not record 100 in both accounts at year-end. A demand deposit contractually earmarked for collateral needs separate IAS 7/IFRIC nature and availability/presentation/disclosure analysis; “restricted” does not automatically mean non-cash under IFRS. US ASC 230 restricted-cash cash-flow reconciliation differs in architecture. Official [IFRIC demand-deposit agenda decision project](https://www.ifrs.org/projects/completed-projects/2022/demand-deposits-with-restrictions-on-use-ias-7/), [FASB ASU 2016-18](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU_2016-18.pdf) and [FRC FRS 102 register](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/accounting-and-reporting/uk-accounting-standards/frs-102/) checked 2026-09-27; period-specific AASB 107/ASC 230/FRS 102 paragraph map remains for approval. Retain direct bank evidence, outstanding-item register, account inventory, transfer pair, restriction agreement and review.

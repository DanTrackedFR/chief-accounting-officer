# TOPIC-13-008 — Complex Contract Accounting Assessment / Related-Party Transaction Accounting

Status: **REVIEWED / production-candidate**  
Primary capabilities: CAO-13-015, CAO-13-016  
Sensitivity: M  
Knowledge types: PRINCIPLES / STANDARDS / PRACTICE

## Purpose
Give the CAO a repeatable method for contracts that cross accounting topics and for transactions involving related parties. The topic is deliberately an orchestration topic: it does not replace revenue, leases, financial instruments, consolidation, compensation or other specialist accounting topics.

## Required context
Entity and reporting perimeter; framework and reporting period; executed contract plus amendments/side letters; counterparties and ownership/governance relationships; transaction economics; consideration and settlement terms; performance/transfer obligations; guarantees/options/contingencies; approvals; prior accounting; materiality; applicable local-law or regulator constraints.

## CAO workflow
1. Establish entity, framework, period and transaction date before selecting literature.
2. Obtain the complete legal/economic arrangement. Do not analyze an isolated invoice or clause where linked contracts, amendments or side letters alter the economics.
3. Identify counterparties and test related-party status before measurement/disclosure conclusions.
4. Decompose the arrangement into accounting units/issues: revenue, lease, financing, financial instrument, employee compensation, asset acquisition/business combination, guarantee, provision/contingency, equity contribution/distribution, intercompany item or other topic.
5. Build a contract-to-guidance matrix. For each issue record facts, applicable topic, recognition unit, measurement basis, timing, presentation, disclosure and unresolved judgments.
6. Resolve interaction/order-of-application questions explicitly. A standard that specifically governs an element is applied before generic policy reasoning.
7. Assess substance, side agreements, circular flows, non-cash consideration, off-market terms and whether multiple contracts should be evaluated together under the applicable topic.
8. For related parties, distinguish (a) relationship identification, (b) accounting measurement/recognition under the transaction-specific standard, and (c) related-party disclosure. Related-party status does not itself create one universal measurement basis.
9. Trace balances, commitments, guarantees and settlement activity to the GL/subledgers and financial-statement disclosures.
10. Produce the conclusion memo, entries/calculations, disclosure support, control evidence and durable Company Accounting Memory candidates.

## Related-party standards routing
### IFRS
IAS 24 is the disclosure anchor. Its objective is to make users aware that financial position or performance may have been affected by related-party relationships, transactions, outstanding balances and commitments. A related-party transaction is a transfer of resources, services or obligations regardless of whether a price is charged. Parent/subsidiary relationships are disclosed even without transactions; transactions, balances and commitments require the specified disclosures when applicable. Key-management compensation is separately addressed. Claims that terms are equivalent to arm's-length terms require substantiation. Recognition and measurement normally route to the IFRS Standard governing the underlying transaction rather than IAS 24 alone.

### US GAAP
Route related-party disclosure to ASC 850 and the underlying transaction to the relevant Codification topic. Public FASB material confirms disclosure of material related-party transactions and the relationship/amount architecture. Current paragraph-level Codification mapping remains **PARTIAL** where the public source path does not expose the full authoritative body; do not manufacture paragraph references.

### UK GAAP
FRS 102 Section 33 is the related-party disclosure anchor. Apply the reporting-period/version gate and entity-size/legal overlays. For periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026, the FRC confirms UK small-entity related-party disclosure routing through 1AC.35 to paragraphs 33.9 and 33.14, subject to specified exemptions, and no paragraph 33.7 KMP-compensation requirement for that small-entity route. FRS 102 also contains a wholly-owned-entity transaction disclosure exemption tied to company law. Underlying recognition/measurement remains governed by the relevant FRS 102 section.

### AASB
AASB 124 incorporates IAS 24 with Australian-specific paragraphs. For-profit Tier 1 entities complying with AASB 124 also comply with IAS 24; Australian entity type and reporting tier must still be resolved. The current AASB compilation identifies parent/ultimate-control disclosures, KMP compensation, related-party transactions/balances/commitments and Australian-specific requirements. Tier 2 disclosure routing and NFP/public-sector overlays must be assessed rather than inferred from IFRS.

## Key framework differences / gates
- Definitions and disclosure architecture are similar across IFRS/AASB and broadly comparable in objective to US/UK requirements, but do not assume identical scopes, exemptions or disclosure detail.
- UK company-law/entity-size exemptions can materially change required disclosure.
- AASB has Australian-specific paragraphs and NFP/public-sector guidance; entity type/tier is a mandatory input.
- US conclusions require independent ASC routing; IFRS paragraph references are never proxies for ASC authority.
- Related-party status is a disclosure/governance signal, not a shortcut to fair value, cost, or another measurement basis. Determine measurement from the transaction-specific literature.

## Complex-contract decision logic
Create an issue tree before conclusions:
- What rights and obligations exist and when are they enforceable?
- Are there multiple contracts or side agreements that must be considered together?
- Is any consideration contingent, variable, non-cash or financed?
- Does the arrangement contain a lease, derivative, guarantee, financing component, repurchase/option or embedded service?
- Is a counterparty an employee, shareholder, parent, subsidiary, associate, JV, KMP or controlled by a relevant person?
- Does the arrangement transfer an asset/business, provide goods/services, compensate employees, finance the entity, or distribute/contribute equity?
- Which accounting topic governs each element and what is the required order of application?
- Are terms off-market and, if so, does another accounting element explain the difference?
- What disclosure, approval, conflict-of-interest and evidence requirements follow?

## Documentation package
Minimum: executed agreement set; relationship/ownership analysis; issue tree; contract-to-guidance matrix; key judgments; calculations; entries; related-party register linkage; approval/conflict evidence; disclosure checklist and tie-out; open-items log. Material or unusual arrangements require a technical memo with alternatives considered and evidence supporting substance.

## Controls / audit / systems
Controls should cover contract completeness, amendment/side-letter capture, related-party master data, annual/trigger-based declarations, approval of conflicts, accounting review of unusual contracts, mapping to specialist topics, period-end balance/commitment completeness, disclosure aggregation and financial-statement tie-out. Audit evidence should permit reperformance from executed terms to accounting conclusion and disclosure.

Systems should maintain counterparty identifiers across ERP/procurement/CRM/equity/legal systems and preserve contract/version lineage. A recurring cross-system related-party or complex-contract reconciliation may be a TrackedFR candidate only where it genuinely requires repeated multi-system data extraction/reconciliation/manipulation; ordinary memo drafting is not.

## Completion outputs
- contract/accounting issue map;
- related-party determination and register update;
- framework-specific recognition/measurement routing;
- entries/calculations or specialist-topic handoffs;
- disclosure support and tie-out;
- judgment/technical memo where material;
- controls and evidence requirements;
- Company Accounting Memory candidates.

## Recognition principle
Revenue is recognized when/as the relevant performance obligation is satisfied through transfer of control. For each performance obligation, determine at inception whether the over-time criteria are met. If none are met, recognition is at a point in time.

### IFRS / AASB over-time routing
IFRS 15.35 / AASB 15.35 architecture has three routes: customer simultaneously receives/consumes benefits; customer controls an asset as it is created/enhanced; or the asset has no alternative use to the entity and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date. IFRIC's real-estate agenda work confirms that enforceability depends on contractual terms plus applicable law/legal precedent and that likelihood of exercising a right is not the test.

For an over-time obligation, select a method that faithfully depicts progress. Output or input methods may be appropriate depending on facts; update progress estimates as circumstances change and address whether inputs depict performance rather than inefficiency/materials not yet transferred.

For point-in-time obligations, determine when control transfers using the applicable control indicators and contract facts rather than invoice/cash timing alone.

## Contract modifications — decision model
1. Identify approved change in enforceable rights/obligations and price.
2. Determine whether added promised goods/services are distinct.
3. Determine whether added consideration reflects appropriate standalone selling price, including permitted adjustments.
4. If separate-contract criteria are met, account prospectively as a separate contract.
5. If remaining goods/services are distinct from those already transferred but separate-contract criteria are not met, account prospectively as termination of old + creation of new contract, reallocating remaining consideration as required.
6. If remaining goods/services are not distinct and form part of a partially satisfied single performance obligation, update the transaction price/progress on a cumulative catch-up basis.
7. Mixed modifications require analysis of the affected promises/performance obligations rather than one blanket treatment.

## Framework routing
IFRS 15 and AASB 15 are closely aligned for these core principles, subject to current-version/Australian overlay checks. ASC 606 was jointly developed around the same core revenue model, but the CAO must use current Codification for US paragraph-level conclusions. Revised FRS 102 Section 23, principally effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2026, is proportionately aligned to IFRS 15 but is not assumed identical; pre-2026 periods route to the applicable prior Section 23.

## Required inputs
Executed contract/amendments; enforceability; performance obligations; delivery/service evidence; acceptance clauses; title/possession terms; alternative-use restrictions; termination rights; right-to-payment terms; applicable legal environment where material; modification approval date; added goods/services; SSP support; transaction price/allocation; measure-of-progress data.

## Accounting consequences
Timing conclusion drives revenue, contract asset/liability and cut-off. Modification treatment can be prospective, separate-contract, or cumulative catch-up and can therefore create a material current-period adjustment.

## Journal logic
Over-time: recognize revenue and contract asset/receivable as performance occurs under the governing billing/right-to-consideration model. Point-in-time: recognize upon control transfer. Modification catch-up: record the cumulative change required by revised transaction price/progress. Exact debit/credit depends on billing, receivable and contract-balance facts.

## Controls / audit / systems
Contract and amendment completeness; performance evidence; legal/enforceability review trigger; acceptance evidence; SSP governance; modification queue; progress-data completeness/accuracy; contract-to-billing-to-GL reconciliation; period-end unbilled/deferred review; documented judgment approval.

Systems should preserve contract/version ID, modification effective date, promises/POs, SSP, allocation, recognition pattern, progress metric, billing, contract balances and source evidence.

## Scenarios
1. SaaS annual subscription delivered continuously: test over-time route and straight-line/output depiction, not invoice date.
2. Customized asset with no alternative use but weak cancellation compensation: do not assume over-time; establish enforceable right to payment.
3. Added distinct units at SSP: separate-contract candidate.
4. Added distinct units at discounted price not reflecting SSP: prospective termination/new-contract model may apply.
5. Scope/price change to a partially completed single integrated service: cumulative catch-up candidate.
6. Customer acceptance clause: determine whether substantive control condition or protective/formal clause.
7. UK period beginning 2025: do not apply revised 2026 Section 23 automatically.
8. US conclusion requiring exact paragraph citation: source-depth flag remains until current Codification verification.

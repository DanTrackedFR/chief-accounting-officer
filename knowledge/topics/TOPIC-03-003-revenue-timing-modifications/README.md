## Recognition principle
Revenue is recognized when/as the relevant performance obligation is satisfied through transfer of control. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-US-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-UK-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R004] For each performance obligation, determine at inception whether the over-time criteria are met. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R001] [TOPIC-03-003-US-R001] [TOPIC-03-003-UK-R001] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R001] If none are met, recognition is at a point in time. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-US-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-UK-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R004]

### IFRS / AASB over-time routing
IFRS 15.35 / AASB 15.35 architecture has three routes: customer simultaneously receives/consumes benefits; customer controls an asset as it is created/enhanced; or the asset has no alternative use to the entity and the entity has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R002] [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R001] IFRIC's real-estate agenda work confirms that enforceability depends on contractual terms plus applicable law/legal precedent and that likelihood of exercising a right is not the test. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R002] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R002]

For an over-time obligation, select a method that faithfully depicts progress. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R001] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R001] [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R005] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R005] Output or input methods may be appropriate depending on facts; update progress estimates as circumstances change and address whether inputs depict performance rather than inefficiency/materials not yet transferred. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R005] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R005]

For point-in-time obligations, determine when control transfers using the applicable control indicators and contract facts rather than invoice/cash timing alone. [TOPIC-03-003-IFRS-R004] [TOPIC-03-003-AASB-R004]

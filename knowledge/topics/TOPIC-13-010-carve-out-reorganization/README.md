# TOPIC-13-010 — Carve-Out Financial Statements / Fresh-Start & Reorganization Accounting Assessment

Status: REVIEWED / production-candidate
Primary capabilities: CAO-13-019, CAO-13-020
Sensitivity: H

## Purpose
Enable the CAO to build a defensible accounting workplan for carve-out financial statements and assess whether a restructuring/reorganization creates a new reporting entity, predecessor/successor presentation, fresh-start accounting or another transaction-specific accounting consequence.

## CAO gates
1. Identify reporting objective, filing/jurisdiction, framework, entities, periods and transaction date.
2. Define the historical business perimeter and legal/entity changes. Do not equate legal perimeter with accounting reporting perimeter.
3. Establish which assets, liabilities, revenue, expenses, cash flows, employees, contracts and shared services are specifically attributable.
4. Identify allocations from parent/shared functions and require a rational, supportable, consistently applied methodology.
5. Reconcile carve-out balances to source entity ledgers and parent consolidated reporting; preserve eliminations and intercompany history.
6. Assess transaction form separately: common control, business combination, asset transfer, spin/demerger, insolvency/reorganization or other.
7. Route recognition/measurement to the applicable transaction literature; do not invent a universal "carve-out GAAP".
8. Assess predecessor/successor and fresh-start requirements only where the applicable framework/jurisdiction actually provides them.
9. Build disclosures explaining basis of preparation, allocations, related parties, dependencies, judgments and non-comparability.
10. Maintain an evidence map from financial-statement line to source ledger/allocation/elimination.

## Framework architecture
### IFRS / AASB
There is no single IFRS/AASB carve-out standard. Apply the applicable IFRS/AASB recognition and measurement requirements to the defined reporting entity/perimeter, with IAS 1/IFRS 18 period routing, IAS 8 policy/judgment architecture, IAS 24 related-party disclosure, IFRS 3 where a business combination is in scope, and transaction-specific standards. Common-control scope gaps must be surfaced rather than filled with unsupported acquisition accounting.

### US GAAP
Route to current Codification by transaction and SEC/regulatory context where applicable. Fresh-start accounting is a specific reorganization model, not a generic reset available for ordinary restructurings. Public-source Codification body limitations mean paragraph-level US records remain PARTIAL until authoritative text is available.

### UK GAAP
Route through current FRS 102 including Section 19 business combinations/goodwill, Section 33 related parties and the reporting/presentation requirements applicable to the period. Group-reconstruction/common-control routes can differ materially from IFRS.

## Required artifacts
- perimeter memo and entity map
- source-to-carve-out trial-balance bridge
- allocation methodology and driver evidence
- intercompany/elimination matrix
- accounting-issue register
- predecessor/successor or reorganization assessment where relevant
- disclosure/basis-of-preparation paper
- audit evidence index

## Controls / audit
Completeness of perimeter; source-ledger reconciliation; allocation approval; consistent drivers; elimination review; transaction-date cutover; related-party completeness; disclosure tie-out; change control over historical reruns.

## Systems
Preserve source-system lineage and transformation rules. A recurring multi-entity carve-out with ERP/warehouse/Excel transformations is a candidate for controlled reconciliation automation; TrackedFR is relevant only where recurring cross-system extraction/reconciliation/manipulation is genuinely present.

## Completion test
CAO can define the perimeter, reconstruct historical reporting, challenge allocations, route transaction accounting, prepare evidence and disclosures, and explain unresolved framework/jurisdiction questions without treating carve-out or fresh-start accounting as a single universal model.

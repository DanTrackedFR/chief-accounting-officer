# Error correction and opening-balance method — TOPIC-02-009

Capabilities: CAO-02-019 (identify and correct error), CAO-02-020 (opening/rollforward integrity). This extends the preserved README, factory, alternate same-ID paper and earlier bridge. Claims in brackets map to `../standards-claims.json`; the register, rather than an unqualified citation, records authority and period confidence. Prepared 2026-09-29.

## 1. Scope and fact lock

Create a dated case for every suspected misstatement. Record entity, reporting framework and edition, regulator/filer status, transaction and account, period of origin, originally issued/authorized statements (including quarters), discovery date, and source evidence available at original issue. Preserve the original trial balance, subledger, financial statement, XBRL, approval and audit snapshots. A later settlement is evidence to analyze; it is not by itself proof the earlier estimate was wrong. A known invoice omitted by a formula or an available contract term misapplied points to error. Genuinely new information that revises a reasonable previous estimate is generally prospective; a switch between permissible policies has a distinct transition and disclosure analysis [US-01, IFRS-01, UK-01, AU-01]. Route substantive measurement of the underlying accrual, revenue, tax or asset to its technical topic; this method decides timing and correction of a *proven* error.

Ask: Was the prior statement issued? What reliable facts existed then? Was the accounting method permissible? Did management use the information correctly? Was a later event indicative of old conditions or new conditions? Was the item intentionally misstated? Do not relabel an error as a policy or estimate change to avoid a comparative correction. Record alternative conclusions and rejecting evidence.

## 2. Quantify and assess materiality

For each affected annual and interim period, build original → correction → corrected columns for every primary statement line, note, tax, EPS, covenant measure and segment measure. Trace all counterparties and journal entries. A classification-only error can still be material; a zero net income effect is not a safe harbor. Consider the individual item and all detected/uncorrected errors, both current and cumulative effects, qualitative factors, trends and intentionality. A material error in an already issued period goes through prior-period correction; discovery before authorization belongs in the original statements. [IFRS-02, UK-02, AU-02, US-02, SEC-01, SEC-02]

For a US SEC registrant, calculate both the *rollover* effect on period earnings and the *iron-curtain* effect of correcting the ending balance sheet. Determine materiality to **each** previously issued annual/interim statement and to the current statement if the error is corrected now or left uncorrected. SAB 99 rejects a mechanical percentage rule; SAB 108 requires both measures and consideration of all affected statements and disclosures. Keep qualitative judgments independent of a numerical threshold. [SEC-01, SEC-02]

## 3. Framework correction and presentation

| Framework/period | Prior-period error route | Presentation and disclosure decision |
|---|---|---|
| IFRS, IAS 8 (2026 annual periods before IFRS 18 mandatory application) | Material error: restate the comparative period of origin, or opening assets/liabilities/equity of earliest comparative if earlier. If period-specific or cumulative effects cannot practicably be established after reasonable effort, use the earliest practicable date and document why; do not use hindsight. [IFRS-02, IFRS-03] | Correct relevant statement lines, opening equity, cash flow and EPS where applicable; disclose nature, by-period line/EPS effects, earliest opening adjustment and impracticability. Assess IAS 1 additional opening statement of financial position trigger separately; tax under IAS 12. [IFRS-03] |
| AASB 108, relevant Australian compilation | Same core retrospective architecture under paragraphs 41–49, with explicit no-hindsight/impracticability route. The AASB 108 compilation inspected covers annual periods from 1 January 2023; confirm the edition at the case date, particularly AASB 18 changes from 2027. [AU-02, AU-03] | Paragraph 49 line/EPS/opening disclosures for applicable Tier 1 entities. Tier 2 entities using AASB 1060 do **not** apply AASB 108.49; use the applicable simplified disclosure requirements instead. Tax under AASB 112. [AU-03] |
| UK FRS 102 September 2024 edition, principally periods from 1 January 2026 | Section 10.19–10.22: material error retrospective to extent practicable, earliest practicable opening if period effects unavailable. Verify 2025/early-adoption edition, FRS 105/101 or SORP instead of automatically assuming FRS 102. [UK-01, UK-02] | Section 10.23 nature, affected line/period and earliest opening correction or impracticability. Small-entity/company-law comparative disclosures need a separate check under Section 1A and applicable law. [UK-03] |
| US GAAP ASC 250 | Determine materiality and restate previously issued statements for a material error; distinguish prior-period adjustment from correcting a current-period error. Error-specific paragraph candidates are ASC 250-10-45-23 through 45-27, **PROVISIONAL** until current Basic View audit. Do not substitute 45-5 through 45-7 (principle-change mechanics) as error authority. [US-01, US-02] | Reconstruct each affected comparative, prior opening retained earnings/other equity, income-tax and EPS effects; document error and effects. Candidate ASC 250-10-50-7 through 50-10, and interim 50-11/50-12, are **PROVISIONAL**; read current text and entity/period conditions before treating as exact operative references. [US-03, US-04] |

The frameworks share a retrospective material-error concept, but differ in paragraph source, jurisdictional filing obligations, Tier 2/small-entity disclosures, interim reporting and effective-period routing. IFRS/AASB/UK impracticability is a narrow evidence-based exception; do not import it into the US route without checking US GAAP. IFRS 18's changes to IAS 8 title and presentation requirements apply for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027 (earlier adoption permitted); version the IFRS and Australian paper accordingly. [IFRS-04, AU-03, UK-01, US-02]

## 4. US SEC decision tree after GAAP measurement

1. Prior issued statements **materially wrong**: authorized board/committee/officers document the non-reliance date; Big R reissuance restatement. File Form 8-K Item 4.02(a) within four business days of that conclusion for an applicable registrant, stating affected periods, known facts and auditor discussions. Coordinate prompt 10-K/A or 10-Q/A corrections, subsequent filings, incorporation and auditor reports with counsel. The trigger is the authorized conclusion, not mere discovery. [SEC-03]
2. Prior issued statements individually reliable, but a current-period adjustment **or** leaving the error uncorrected would materially misstate the current report: little r revision of comparative amounts when next presented, with disclosure. Generally no Item 4.02(a) because the old statements remain reliable; evaluate pending filings, offerings and any independent auditor notice. [SEC-04]
3. Both prior and current effects immaterial, individually and with other errors: a documented current out-of-period adjustment may be acceptable. Monitor accumulation and trends; deliberate manipulation can be material despite a small amount. [SEC-01, SEC-02, SEC-04]
4. If the auditor independently advises that its prior report/review should not be relied upon, test Item 4.02(b)/(c), including copy to accountant, accountant letter and 8-K amendment deadline. An issuer that already filed 4.02(a) on the same matter generally need not duplicate the 4.02(b) filing; a different auditor matter is different. Test ICFR, disclosure controls, certifications, clawback for listed issuers, covenants and exchange requirements for either restatement route. [SEC-03, SEC-05]

This is a US domestic Form 8-K route. Foreign private issuers and other regimes require their own form/filing analysis. A live case requires counsel and auditor engagement; the method supplies the decision evidence, not a legal filing determination.

## 5. Posting, systems and approval

Do not overwrite issued ledgers. Keep immutable original and restated reporting versions with period, entity, account, dimension, journal, source document and approving person. Post an approved correction in the open ledger or a controlled restatement layer according to system design; reconcile it to each restated comparative and next opening balance. For an error predating the earliest comparative, the cumulative after-tax correction runs through opening equity with corresponding assets/liabilities. For an error within a presented comparative, restate that comparative's P&L/cash-flow and closing balance; do not assign its entire effect to earliest opening equity. Tax recognition/classification and EPS must be computed, not assumed. Reconcile prior approved close + approved restatement/reclass + legitimate perimeter/FX/mapping = current opening, both at TB and subledger detail. A balanced TB with misallocated customers still fails. [IFRS-02, AU-02, UK-02, US-02]

Minimum workpapers: dated fact chronology; framework/version and registrant scope; original evidence; transaction population completeness; line-by-line and period-by-period bridge; tax/EPS/cash-flow calculations; qualitative and aggregate materiality; SAB 99/108 sheets if SEC; proposed original/restated disclosures; Item 4.02 clock and governance minutes if applicable; entries and report lineage; opening/subledger tie; auditor communications; root cause, deficiency severity and control remediation. Controller prepares, technical accounting challenges classification and calculations, tax validates tax, CAO approves, disclosure committee/counsel handles filing, audit committee oversees non-reliance. Escalate a conflict rather than forcing a clean reconciliation.

Controls: completeness of identified errors and source populations; locked historical version; independent dual-measure materiality review; restricted reopening/posting permissions; period/entity mapping approval; journal-to-disclosure tie-out; quarterly refresh of the uncorrected-error log; independent review of exception and root cause; post-filing verification of XBRL and comparative figures.

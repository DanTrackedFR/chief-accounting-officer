# Climate financial-risk connectivity and sustainability data governance — substantive factory

Companion to prior README; CAO-17-004/005. Source checked 2026-09-27. Proposed REVIEWED candidate for finance/data governance method; climate scenario and local disclosure requirements remain case-specific.

## Risk-to-ledger architecture

[IFRS S2](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/) addresses climate-related risks and opportunities, including governance, strategy, risk management, metrics and targets; [IFRS S1](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s1-general-requirements/) emphasizes connected information. AASB S2, UK SRS and ESRS require separate local treatment. No climate narrative independently changes IAS 36 recoverable amount, ASC 360 impairment, a provision or useful life. But credible plans, market conditions and regulations can change their underlying inputs and disclosures. Map physical risk by hazard/site/asset/exposure/horizon and transition risk by policy/price/technology/product/customer/horizon; identify the responsible accounting balance and forecast owner. A risk can affect cash flows, discount rates, asset life, credit loss, decommissioning costs, insurance and liquidity, sometimes in opposite directions. Preserve measurement-objective differences.

## Data governance and source quality

Maintain a metric register: definition, entity and site boundary, reporting unit, observed versus estimated, data owner, acquisition date, source system/document, factor/model version, fiscal period, correction policy, reviewer, retention and disclosure location. Reconcile the population first (sites, meters, suppliers, trips), then numeric values. Late electricity bills need cutoff or estimation with later true-up and a recorded `event_date` versus `received_date`. Lock factors for a reported period; a later factor update must not silently overwrite prior released disclosures. Record missing data, substitution method, uncertainty, sensitivity and escalation. Climate risk and accounting teams should share an assumption inventory but need not use one artificially identical scenario for different purposes.

## Worked finance connection

Site A's transition plan expects closure in 2030, while its equipment has a depreciation life ending 2045 and carrying amount 8m. Management's closure decision may shorten the useful life, trigger impairment or decommissioning evaluation, or remain contingent; test board approvals, feasibility, replacement strategy, expected cash flows and probability. If supported remaining useful life falls from 15 to 4 years with no residual value, prospective straight-line annual depreciation on 8m would change from about 0.533m to 2.0m (increment 1.467m), subject to actual available-for-use/carrying facts. This calculation does not assert that the plan is sufficiently committed or that an impairment exists. Route to TOPIC-04-002/006 and 15-003; document the climate disclosure's scenario horizon versus booked base case.

## Controls and challenges

Control ownership and precision at each extract; reconcile site master to facilities/leases/PPE; reconcile modeled capex to approved budget; obtain risk-owner sign-off; compare climate scenario assumptions to impairment, fair value, ECL, going concern and forecasts each reporting period. Maintain an exceptions matrix with cause, quantified effect, authorized difference and remediation. S1: newly acquired plant missing from emissions but included in group PPE → completeness fail. S2: 2030 closure ignored by impairment team → require documented challenge, not automatic entry. S3: updated energy-price forecast alters climate scenario but not approved financial forecast → explain basis and sensitivity. S4: estimate overwritten after report publication without version history → fail audit trail. Outputs: boundary/data dictionary, risk-to-balance map, assumptions bridge, sensitivity memo, review record and disclosure reconciliation. Residual: case-specific climate data and measurement estimates require evidence; method is reviewable.

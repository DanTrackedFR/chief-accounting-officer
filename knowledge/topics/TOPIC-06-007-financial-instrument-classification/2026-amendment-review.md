# 2026 financial-instrument classification update

Capabilities CAO-06-014/015. Both existing 06-007 paths retained; canonicalization pending independent QA. Financial-asset classification under IFRS 9/AASB 9 requires both business-model evidence and contractual cash-flow characteristics. A loan nominally held for collection with a return contingent on a borrower's carbon target cannot be assigned amortized cost from its “loan” label. Analyze whether contingent contractual flows satisfy the applicable SPPI conditions, including the May 2024 amendments effective for annual periods beginning 1 January 2026; document the pre/post-effective-period treatment and transition. A portfolio managed on fair-value performance cannot be assumed hold-to-collect merely because some instruments reach maturity.

Assume a bond bought for 980, coupon 40 and expected principal 1,000, but a contract clause increases the final payment by 300 if an unrelated commodity benchmark rises. Before any EIR schedule, obtain full terms and assess whether cash flows are solely principal/interest under applicable IFRS/AASB requirements. If not, the amortized-cost illustration is invalid and fair-value-through-profit-or-loss routing may be required; do not assume the 300 will never pay. Financial liability classification separately requires issuer obligation and settlement alternatives; convertible instruments may have framework-specific liability/equity split, derivative and fair-value consequences. ASC 320/321/825/480 and FRS 102 Sections 11/12 do not use an identical IFRS 9 business-model/SPPI scheme.

Official [IASB May 2024 classification/measurement amendment](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/news/2024/05/iasb-issues-amendments-cmfi-ifrs7-ifrs9/) and [IFRS 9 overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-9-financial-instruments/) checked 2026-09-27. Amendment also addresses electronic payment system settlement and disclosures; test relevant cash/payment flows rather than limiting update to ESG-linked loans. Preserve signed terms, cash-flow scenarios, portfolio management reports, election rationale, valuation, ledger classification and disclosures.

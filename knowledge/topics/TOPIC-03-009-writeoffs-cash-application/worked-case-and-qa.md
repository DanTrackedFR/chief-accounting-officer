# Write-off, recovery and cash-application case

Capabilities CAO-03-018/019. Supplements both existing duplicate paths without deleting either. A write-off conclusion depends on recoverability evidence, not days overdue alone. The receivable and allowance below are in one currency; no tax, FX or interest assumed.

## Decision and postings

A customer owes 120. At close, the approved loss allowance attributable to this invoice is 90. A liquidator reports that only 20 will be recovered and the remaining 100 has no reasonable recovery expectation. Under an IFRS 9/AASB 9 write-off analysis, remove gross AR of 100, use 90 of allowance and recognize the remaining 10 loss if not already provided: Dr allowance 90, Dr impairment expense 10, Cr AR 100. The surviving 20 remains subject to impairment and collection monitoring. Do not write off all 120 merely because insolvency proceedings began.

Later, if 5 of the written-off amount is unexpectedly recovered, link receipt to the original customer and write-off register. An illustrative cash entry is Dr cash 5 / Cr impairment recovery or other account under the entity's framework-consistent policy; confirm whether reinstating AR is needed in the subledger workflow. Keep the original write-off history. **Executed:** 90+10=100; 120−100=20; 5 recovery is not new revenue. PASS arithmetic and classification under assumed evidence.

## One-to-many remittance

A bank receipt of 250 references invoices A 100, B 80 and C 70; the customer claims a 10 SLA deduction on C. Apply 100+80+60=240 to supported invoice amounts and hold the excess cash of 10 as an unapplied credit pending remittance clarification. Separately retain 10 open on C as a disputed debit until a valid service credit is established. These two 10 balances are not netted merely because they have the same value; they may arise from an erroneous payment instruction. Match the exact bank amount first, then the remittance. **Executed:** 100+80+60=240 applied; 250−240=10 unapplied; C 70−60=10 disputed. PASS detection, dispositions pending evidence.

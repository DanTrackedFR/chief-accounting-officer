# TOPIC-09-009 — Accounting Governance & Approval Matrix / Fraud-Risk Accounting Controls

Status: **REVIEWED / production-candidate**  
Primary capabilities: CAO-09-021, CAO-09-022  
Knowledge type: PRINCIPLES + PRACTICE + JURISDICTION OVERLAY

## Purpose
Enable the CAO to design accounting decision rights and approvals that match financial-reporting risk, and a fraud-risk control layer focused on fraudulent financial reporting and accounting-relevant misappropriation without expanding into enterprise fraud/security ownership.

## Authoritative anchors
PCAOB AS 2401 recognizes management override as an inherent fraud risk and requires audit procedures over journal entries/adjustments, estimate bias and significant unusual transactions. AS 2201 notes that ICFR can be circumvented by collusion or management override, so safeguards reduce but cannot eliminate risk. SEC ICFR guidance uses a top-down risk-based model. These US audit/issuer anchors inform practice; formal obligations remain jurisdiction/entity specific.

## Accounting governance model
Define decision rights across:
- accounting policy and elections;
- significant estimates/judgments;
- manual/non-routine journals;
- account/reconciliation certification;
- close/reopen/late adjustments;
- master data with accounting impact;
- write-offs/credits/reserves;
- related-party/unusual transactions;
- financial statements/disclosures;
- control exceptions/remediation;
- system/report changes affecting accounting.

An approval matrix must specify **what decision**, **risk/threshold**, **preparer/requestor**, **approver(s)**, **required expertise**, **evidence**, **system enforcement**, **delegation rules**, **conflict rules**, **emergency path** and **retrospective review**. Monetary thresholds alone are insufficient: a zero-dollar policy election or unusual related-party arrangement can be high risk.

## Fraud-risk accounting control model
### Fraudulent financial reporting
Consider revenue manipulation, premature/late recognition, fictitious entries, reserve/estimate bias, capitalization manipulation, concealment through suspense/intercompany, management override, omission/misleading disclosures, related-party concealment and unusual transactions.

### Accounting-relevant asset misappropriation
Focus on risks that affect books/reporting: fictitious vendors, unauthorized payments/refunds/credits, payroll ghost employees, write-off abuse, inventory/asset diversion and cash skimming where accounting controls are part of prevention/detection. Route physical security, investigations and enterprise fraud operations to appropriate functions while preserving accounting evidence and consequences.

### Control responses
Use layered prevention/detection: SoD/access; independent approvals; master-data governance; journal restrictions/analytics; estimate governance/back-testing; unusual-transaction review; related-party declarations/matching; reconciliations; vendor/payment change verification; write-off monitoring; whistleblower/escalation linkage where relevant; disclosure consistency checks.

## Management override safeguards
Privileged access and senior approval do not reduce override risk by themselves. Identify users capable of bypassing workflow; monitor their entries/changes independently; preserve immutable logs where possible; require retrospective review of emergency overrides; challenge unusual transactions for business purpose and accounting substance.

## Approval effectiveness
An approver needs authority, competence, information and time. Evidence should show the attributes reviewed and disposition of exceptions. Rubber-stamp approval is an operating failure even if workflow status says approved.

## Required / recommended / world-class
**Required:** governance/fraud controls required by applicable law/regulation and controls necessary for reliable reporting.  
**Recommended:** accounting delegation matrix; non-monetary risk triggers; conflict/delegation rules; override log; fraud-risk linkage in RCM; annual refresh based on incidents/process change.  
**World-class:** approvals dynamically route by risk attributes; privileged actions continuously monitored; unusual transactions scored and surfaced; estimate back-testing feeds fraud/control risk; governance decisions captured in Company Accounting Memory.  
**Shortcut/risk:** CEO/CFO approval treated as universal compensating control; thresholds with no qualitative triggers; undocumented delegations; shared credentials; emergency overrides never retrospectively reviewed.

## CAO execution
1. Resolve company structure/regulatory context.
2. Inventory accounting decisions and current authorities.
3. Map risks, assertions and fraud scenarios.
4. Design monetary + qualitative approval triggers.
5. Test SoD, delegation and privileged bypass paths.
6. Add independent monitoring for override/collusion-sensitive areas.
7. Define evidence and exception governance.
8. Link to RCM, policies, systems and close.
9. Produce remediation and durable governance records.

## Artifacts
Accounting approval/delegation matrix; fraud-risk accounting matrix; management-override register; unusual-transaction review template; privileged-action monitoring spec; related-party accounting control checklist; governance decision log.

## Scenario tests
1. CFO can approve own non-routine journal: **FAIL independence for override-sensitive entry**; require independent authorized review.
2. $1 related-party transaction changes disclosure conclusion: **QUALITATIVE APPROVAL TRIGGER** despite immaterial amount.
3. Vendor bank change and payment release performed by same user: **FRAUD/SoD GAP**; prevent or independently verify/monitor.
4. Emergency close override is logged but never reviewed: **INCOMPLETE CONTROL**; retrospective independent review required.

## Completion criteria
PASS when CAO can build decision-rights governance beyond dollar thresholds, connect fraud scenarios to accounting controls, design management-override safeguards and preserve jurisdiction boundaries.

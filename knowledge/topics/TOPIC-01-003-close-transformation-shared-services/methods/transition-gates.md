# Close transformation and shared services — execution gates

This extends the existing README and Phase 2D completion note; it does not replace their operating model. Knowledge type: PRINCIPLES / PRACTICE. Capabilities: CAO-01-007 and CAO-01-008. Prepared 2026-09-27.

## Baseline, target and ownership

Measure at least three closes at task level: planned and actual start/end, predecessor, entity, account, preparer, reviewer, work hours, waiting hours, late adjustment and evidence defects. Construct the critical path from observed dependencies, not the calendar's nominal dates. Baseline quality at the same time: unsupported reconciling items, reopening, manual journal corrections and auditor findings. A faster close that shifts work into the next month fails the objective.

For a shared service, separate process execution from accounting accountability. The service can prepare an AP accrual, but the entity controller owns whether the service was received in the reporting period and signs the material estimate. The provider or shared-service lead owns completeness of its transaction queue, delivery cutoff, staffing, escalation and evidence. The retained CAO owns policy, material judgment, consolidated control design and final certification. Record each handoff in a service catalogue: input, cutoff/timezone, completeness proof, output, quality criterion, issue owner, escalation SLA, local statutory reviewer and fallback.

## Transformation acceptance workpaper

| Gate | Pass evidence | Failure response |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Three closes with timed dependencies and quality metrics | Repeat measurement before promising target duration |
| Design | Future-state map, retained/shared RACI, source-to-GL interfaces and evidence location | Resolve unowned judgment or data handoff |
| Parallel close | Old and new outputs reconciled by entity/account, all exceptions assigned | Continue double run; no silent cutover |
| First live close | Complete input populations, reconciled GL, independent review and late-adjustment log | Activate fallback and document period impact |
| Stabilization | Repeated closes meet speed **and** quality criteria, contingency staff trained | Extend hypercare or revise model |

Illustration: AP sends 900 invoices by day 2, but 30 late receipts worth 45,000 arrive on day 4. A day-3 lock can be acceptable only if a controlled receipt/accrual estimate captures the unbilled population, subsequent actuals are compared to it and material variances have a reopen or correction route. Merely moving AP's cutoff to day 2 hides the 45,000 and fails. Preserve receipt IDs, estimate method, journal ID, reviewer, true-up and root-cause evidence.

## Framework and source boundary

This is an operational design; no IFRS, FASB, FRC or AASB standard prescribes a universal close duration or shared-service structure. Each underlying accrual, revenue, estimate and error follows its own framework and period. US-issuer ICFR considerations may invoke [SEC Release 33-8810](https://www.sec.gov/rule-release/33-8810); PCAOB evidence considerations are auditor-side under [AS 1105](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS1105). These references were checked 2026-09-27 and are not universal company obligations. Privacy, employment and statutory local-accountability requirements require jurisdiction-specific confirmation.

## Executed challenge and dependency

Challenge: shared-service SLA says 98% tickets closed, while late accounting entries rise from 2 to 18. Outcome **FAIL**: ticket closure is not reconciled posting. Require per-entity source-to-GL completeness, reopening analysis and remediation before declaring transformation success. Dependencies: TOPIC-02-001 close architecture, TOPIC-09-005 close controls, TOPIC-11-003 interface lineage and TOPIC-12-009 service levels. Independent QA still validates this operational conclusion.

# TOPIC-04-011 — sale-and-leaseback subsequent-period workpaper

This is a targeted extension to the existing README and `completion-qa.md`, not a copy of reviewed 04-010. Capabilities CAO-04-025/026. IFRS 16's [sale-and-leaseback amendment](https://www.ifrs.org/projects/completed-projects/2022/lease-liability-in-a-sale-and-leaseback/) and [AASB 2022-5](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/related-documents?pronouncement=3655) apply from annual periods beginning 1 January 2024, subject to adoption/transition verification. [FRS 102](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/accounting-and-reporting/uk-accounting-standards/frs-102/) edition and [ASC 842](https://asc.fasb.org/) require separate period and paragraph-level review; no generic IFRS gain is transported to those frameworks.

## Worked bridge following the existing example

First establish that transfer of control occurred under the relevant sale model. The existing simplified IFRS example has 1,000,000 fair-value consideration, 600,000 pre-sale carrying amount, 400,000 present value of retained lease rights, initial ROU 240,000, liability 400,000 and gain limited to 240,000. Opening seller-lessee balance sheet therefore removes original PPE 600,000, adds ROU 240,000, cash 1,000,000 and lease liability 400,000; the income statement gain is 240,000. The entry balances at 1,240,000 on each side.

For a **separate illustrative first period** assume a contractually supported 5% periodic interest rate and a 100,000 end-of-period fixed payment, no reassessment, FX or variable amounts. Liability = 400,000 + 20,000 interest − 100,000 payment = 320,000. Dr interest expense 20,000 / Cr liability 20,000; Dr liability 100,000 / Cr cash 100,000. If a separately supported five-period straight-line ROU consumption pattern applies, Dr depreciation 48,000 / Cr accumulated ROU depreciation 48,000, leaving ROU 192,000. These assumptions are pedagogical and do not establish a universal leaseback liability or ROU method; verify the actual payment stream, IFRS amendment's retained-right gain constraint and transition before posting.

## Exception tests and control gate

1. A substantive repurchase option prevents sale qualification under the applicable facts: retain underlying asset and route consideration as financing; no ordinary disposal gain.
2. Consideration deviates from fair value or lease payments from market: identify additional financing or prepayment adjustment before using the simplified ratio.
3. Subsequent variable payments not dependent on an index/rate: **conditional**, not the 320,000 liability result. Obtain the amended IFRS 16 requirements and contract-specific schedule to ensure no gain is recognized on retained use; public summary alone does not support a prescribed calculation.
4. Different UK or US seller-lessee: reperform transfer, classification, initial gain and subsequent schedule under that framework, not this IFRS entry.

Controller reconciles agreement-to-sale memo-to-opening journals, contract terms to rate and schedule, payments to bank, liability/ROU movement to GL and gain/interest/depreciation to notes. Fixed and variable-payment arithmetic, US contrast and UK elections are developed in phase-2d-method.md. Live contract inputs and operative-source precision remain separate application/audit gates.

## Variable-payment amendment route and alternative-framework challenge

The [IFRS Interpretations Committee's June 2020 decision](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2020/ifric-update-june-2020/) explains that the initial retained-use proportion can use the present value of *expected* lease payments, including variable amounts, relative to the transferred asset's fair value in its example. The [IASB's December 2021 project decision](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/iasb/2021/iasb-update-december-2021/) and final 2022 amendment add the subsequent-measurement discipline: the seller-lessee determines lease/revised lease payments in a way that does not recognize gain on its retained right of use. This is distinct from an ordinary lessee's exclusion of some usage-based payments. In a variable-only leaseback, build an inception expectation schedule with source forecast, rate and fair-value support, then compare actual period payments and contract modifications with the adopted payment-determination approach. Reconcile liability unwind and ROU consumption, and separately explain a difference between expected and realized payments. Do not simply reverse the original leaseback liability to gain when variable cash payments differ. The amendment is applied retrospectively to sale-and-leaseback transactions entered after IFRS 16 initial application, subject to its transition provision and the entity's adoption date; retrieve the operative text for a live restatement. The [AASB amendment and applicable version](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/node/3527) require the corresponding Australian edition and tier check.

US [FASB ASU 2016-02](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU%202016-02_Section%20A.pdf) establishes the ASC 842 sale-and-leaseback framework: resolve ASC 606 control and repurchase terms, then the buyer-lessor/seller-lessee lease classification and market adjustments under the applicable period. A transfer that fails the sale criteria is financing, even if cash is described as sale proceeds; never use the IFRS retained-right gain formula for US. The [FRC's February 2026 differences explanation](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/accounting-and-reporting/uk-accounting-standards/uk-accounting-standards-overview/significant-differences-between-frs-102-and-the-ifrs-for-smes-accounting-standard/) identifies revised FRS 102 Section 20's on-balance-sheet model and an optional simplified sale-and-leaseback gain/loss approach. Obtain the operative 2026 Section 20 election and transition for the UK case; pre-2026 sale/finance-lease transactions may carry deferred gain treatment rather than the new election. Adverse test: treating a 2025 UK deferred gain as a 2026 IFRS-style immediate gain without transition evidence fails. Exact current ASC paragraph and unusual UK election calculations still need authorized version-specific review before a live journal is approved.

### Verified source detail and different numerical outcomes

The [AASB 16 sale-and-leaseback compilation operative from 2024](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/node/3643) exposes paragraphs 98–103, including 102A. The subsequent payment selection prevents a later gain on retained use, while a partial/full lease termination is separately analyzed; the amended rule does not require a universal variable-payment forecast model. The [FRC March 2024 amendments](https://media.frc.org.uk/documents/Amendments_to_FRS_102_and_other_FRSs_REDACTED.pdf) expose revised Section 20.123(a)'s consistent policy choice: proportionate previous carrying value or an ROU under its Section 20.46 model with a deferred excess of proceeds over old carrying amount. Applied to the earlier **hypothetical** 1m sale/600k book/400k leaseback liability with no other adjustments, the first UK route reproduces 240k ROU and 240k immediate gain; the second gives 400k ROU, 400k liability and 400k deferred gain, subject to amortization over the supported lease term. Its initial journal would be Dr cash 1m and ROU 400k; Cr original PPE 600k, lease liability 400k and deferred gain 400k. Both sides total 1.4m. A loss when proceeds fall below carrying amount has different immediate treatment and requires the exact fact pattern. Section 20.124–126 covers off-market adjustments and failed sales; Section 20.77 adds relevant sale/leaseback and election disclosure. Check the operative edition and transition, not the amendment draft in isolation.

The original [FASB ASU 2016-02 Topic 842 sale-and-leaseback text](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU%202016-02_Section%20A.pdf) identifies sale-control conditions, finance/sales-type leaseback disqualification, constrained repurchase exceptions, failed-sale financing and the US fair-value/market adjustment route in paragraphs 842-40-25-1–5 and 30-1–3 **as published in 2016**. Assuming a US case meets those conditions and has 1m fair-value proceeds, 600k old carrying amount and 400k initial operating-lease ROU/liability with no other adjustment, the illustrative entry is Dr cash 1m and ROU 400k; Cr PPE 600k, liability 400k and sale gain 400k. Both sides total 1.4m. This contrasts with the IFRS 240k immediate gain and the UK election. The 2016 ASU is historical source verification, not proof the entire 2026 Codification body is unchanged; check authorized current ASC 842 before posting. A repurchase option that fails the ASC exception, a finance leaseback, off-market pricing or variable cash flows invalidate the simple US result.

## Variable-only leaseback: two-period expected-payments workpaper

The [June 2020 IFRS Interpretations Committee decision](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2020/ifric-update-june-2020/) establishes that a seller-lessee can use the present value of expected payments, including revenue-based variable payments, to estimate the retained-use proportion and recognizes a leaseback liability even when every payment is variable. The [2022 amendment](https://www.ifrs.org/projects/completed-projects/2022/lease-liability-in-a-sale-and-leaseback/) and [AASB 16 paragraph 102A](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/node/3643) constrain subsequent measurement so that no gain or loss is recognized on the retained right, except where a partial or full termination is analyzed separately. The [IASB's December 2021 staff illustration, Appendix B](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2021/december/iasb/ap12a-ifrs-16-lease-liability-in-a-sale-and-leaseback.pdf) illustrates an **expected-payments approach**; it predates the final amendment and is explanatory history, not mandatory text. Management must document a compliant payment-determination policy and whether this illustration fits the actual arrangement.

Apply that approach to an invented two-year revenue-percentage leaseback with the same 1,000,000 fair-value sale, 600,000 old carrying value and 400,000 inception present value of *expected* leaseback payments as above. Assume 5% annual discounting, equal forecast cash payments at each year end, no minimum or index-linked cash, no off-market terms, no modifications, a two-year straight-line ROU consumption pattern, and no material rounding. The forecast is 400,000 / (1/1.05 + 1/1.05²) = **215,121.95 per year**. It is an invented forecast, not evidence of a real contract. Initial retained share 40%, ROU 240,000, liability 400,000 and transferred-right gain 240,000 reconcile to the opening entry above. The fact that every contractual payment is variable does **not** zero the resulting sale-and-leaseback liability.

Year 1 illustrative entries: Dr interest 20,000 / Cr liability 20,000; Dr liability 215,121.95 and Dr variable lease expense 24,878.05 / Cr cash 240,000; Dr ROU depreciation 120,000 / Cr accumulated ROU depreciation 120,000. Year 2: Dr interest 10,243.90 / Cr liability; Dr liability 215,121.95 / Cr cash 190,000 and Cr variable lease expense reversal 25,121.95; Dr ROU depreciation 120,000 / Cr accumulated depreciation. Round interest and closing balance at the journal level so the final liability clears. The unfavorable/favorable differences are attributed to current variable lease cash, **not a later gain on rights retained in the sale**. If the documented policy does not support releasing the original expectation in this way, this workpaper fails and the schedule is rebuilt under the approved policy; no claim is made that IFRS requires this specific equal-payment model.

Source/evidence fields in the approval sheet: contract percentage and revenue definition, forecast revenue version and owner, rate support, independent asset valuation, original carrying amount, sale-control and repurchase analysis, adopted expected-payment determination, GL journal IDs, cash/revenue population and a forecast-versus-actual bridge. Tests: zero contractual minimum with a zero opening liability **FAIL**; recognize the 24,878.05 cash difference as an extra sale gain **FAIL**; year-2 liability greater than zero without modification or rounding support **FAIL**; a partial termination sent through this unmodified schedule **FAIL**. IASB/AASB effective date, transaction cohort and retrospective transition remain a reporting-period control. UK election and current US ASC 842 have distinct schedules and cannot inherit these numbers.

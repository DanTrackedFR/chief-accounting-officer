# Sale-and-leaseback and disclosure reconciliation — completion method

Companion to the existing `completion-qa.md`, `applied-qa.md` and 04-010 lease slice; the reviewed lease topic is unchanged. CAO-04-025/026. Claim IDs are in `standards-claims.json`.

## Sale gate before gain

IFRS 16/AASB 16 determine whether control transferred under the revenue sale criteria. If not, keep the underlying asset and record a financing liability for proceeds; the buyer has a financial asset. For a qualifying sale, seller-lessee recognizes an ROU for its retained right and only gain relating to rights transferred, with off-market terms adjusted. Subsequent variable-payment measurement must avoid creating a gain/loss on the retained right except qualifying termination. The 2022 IFRS amendment applies for annual periods beginning 1 January 2024, with specified retrospective treatment for transactions after initial IFRS 16 adoption; AASB adoption/transition is separately checked [04-011-IFRS-01; 04-011-IFRS-02; 04-011-AASB-01].

US ASC 842-40 applies ASC 606 sale control and bars successful sale accounting if the leaseback is finance/sales-type; a repurchase option generally prevents sale except the narrowly defined fair-value/alternative-asset exception. A failed sale remains a financing with continuing asset depreciation and finance-liability accretion; a successful sale generally derecognizes the asset, recognizes the applicable leaseback ROU/liability and sale gain, subject to fair-value/off-market corrections. Variable-only payments that do not depend on an index/rate are generally excluded from the initial US lease liability and expensed when incurred, so the IFRS expected-payment liability cannot be transplanted into US books. Assess lease classification, term and any in-substance fixed amounts first [04-011-US-01; 04-011-US-02].

UK FRS 102 revised Section 20 has a policy choice for a qualifying sale and leaseback: a proportionate retained-right model or an ROU/deferred-gain model, applied consistently and disclosed; failed sale and off-market terms require separate treatment. Document whether Periodic Review 2024 Section 20 is effective or early adopted and its transition, rather than assume a 2025 lease uses revised mechanics [04-011-UK-01].

## Three initial entries, one fact pattern

Assume fair-value sale 1,000,000, prior carrying 600,000, 40% retained-use proportion, initial fixed leaseback liability 400,000, no selling costs or off-market element, successful sale in each framework and operating leaseback for US. IFRS/AASB: retained ROU 240,000 and transferred-right gain (1,000,000 − 600,000)×60% = 240,000. Dr cash 1,000,000 / Dr ROU 240,000 / Cr underlying asset 600,000 / Cr liability 400,000 / Cr gain 240,000. US: Dr cash 1,000,000 / Dr ROU 400,000 / Cr asset 600,000 / Cr liability 400,000 / Cr gain 400,000. UK revised Section 20 proportionate route matches the 240,000/240,000 illustration on stipulated facts; deferred route: Dr cash 1,000,000 / Dr ROU 400,000 / Cr asset 600,000 / Cr liability 400,000 / Cr deferred gain 400,000, then release over supported term. Each initial entry balances 1,240,000 or 1,400,000 as appropriate. These examples do not settle taxes, selling costs or actual contract valuation [04-011-IFRS-01; 04-011-US-01; 04-011-UK-01].

## Variable-only subsequent bridge and US contrast

The existing IFRS/AASB two-year expected-payment example uses inception liability 400,000, 5% rate, equal selected expected payments 215,121.95, year-end balances 204,878.05 and approximately zero. Actual cash 240,000 then 190,000 yields differences +24,878.05 and −25,121.95; reconcile the selected payment release, interest, variable expense adjustment and cash without recording a sale gain on retained rights. A change in contractual scope or partial termination requires separate remeasurement. Management must justify the adopted expected-payment determination; the numerical schedule is one defensible illustrative policy, not mandated by the staff example [04-011-IFRS-02; 04-011-AASB-01].

For a **separate US variable-only case**, stipulate an otherwise successful operating sale, no fixed/in-substance fixed or index-linked lease payments, and no other initial-lease-liability components. Initial liability from those variable payments is zero. Illustrative entry is Dr cash 1,000,000 / Cr asset 600,000 / Cr gain 400,000; subsequent 240,000 and 190,000 revenue-linked rents are lease expense/cash in their periods. Other lease initial direct costs, sale price deviations or prepaid rent would change the entry. If the leaseback is finance classified or repurchase option invalidates the sale, the entire 400,000 gain fails and the sale proceeds finance the retained asset. This counterexample distinguishes US from IFRS rather than forcing the IFRS 400,000 liability into ASC 842 [04-011-US-01; 04-011-US-02].

## Close and challenge controls

Keep sale agreement, market valuation, prior gross/accumulated cost, lease and repurchase clauses, transfer-control memo, lease classification, contract and expected/actual cash schedules, chosen UK policy, sale proceeds bank, discount rate, GL IDs and note crosswalk. Reconcile ROU/liability movements to 04-010 and cash flows, sale gain/deferred balance to P&L/notes, and off-market adjustment to source valuation. Reviewer tests sale-control and classification independently of arithmetic. Adverse tests: repurchase at a fixed price, finance leaseback, below-market consideration, variable payments zeroed in IFRS, US variable rent forced into an inception liability, IFRS retained-right gain from later cash variance, UK deferred gain immediately recognized, and termination processed as ordinary scheduled rent. Each fails the simple examples [04-011-IFRS-02; 04-011-US-02; 04-011-UK-01].

# TOPIC-09-007 — Control Deficiency Evaluation & Remediation Planning

Status: **REVIEWED / production-candidate**  
Primary capabilities: CAO-09-017, CAO-09-018  
Knowledge type: PRINCIPLES + PRACTICE + JURISDICTION OVERLAY

## Purpose
Enable the CAO to evaluate a control failure based on financial-reporting risk, determine severity under the applicable regime, assess compensating controls and design remediation that fixes root cause and is capable of being evidenced and retested.

## Authoritative US-issuer anchor
PCAOB AS 2201.62–.69 provides a rigorous deficiency model: severity depends on the reasonable possibility that controls fail to prevent/detect a misstatement and the magnitude of potential misstatement; it does not depend on whether a misstatement actually occurred. Multiple deficiencies may combine. Compensating controls mitigate only when they operate at sufficient precision. AS 2201 identifies indicators of material weakness including senior-management fraud, certain restatements, auditor-identified material misstatement not detected by ICFR and ineffective audit-committee oversight. SEC guidance likewise emphasizes qualitative and quantitative facts and compensating controls.

Do not automatically apply US labels (`material weakness`, `significant deficiency`) as statutory classifications to non-US entities. The underlying risk analysis remains useful; formal classification/communication follows jurisdiction and assurance regime.

## Deficiency case file
Capture: control ID/objective; account/disclosure/assertion; failure date/period; design vs operating failure; population/time exposed; cause; actual errors found; potential error; likelihood factors; magnitude; compensating controls; aggregation with related deficiencies; reporting/audit consequence; interim mitigation; remediation owner/date; retest evidence; closure approval.

## Evaluation workflow
1. **Validate the failure.** Distinguish missing/poorly designed control from failure of a sound design in operation.
2. **Identify risk exposure.** What misstatement could pass? Which assertions/accounts/disclosures and periods?
3. **Potential magnitude.** Consider exposed population, possible over/understatement, volume, aggregation and qualitative importance; do not cap understatements mechanically at recorded balance.
4. **Likelihood / reasonable possibility.** Assess complexity, susceptibility to fraud/error, judgment, frequency, history and interaction with other controls. Avoid fake probability precision.
5. **Compensating controls.** Verify they address the same risk, operate independently and at sufficient precision, and actually operated during exposure period.
6. **Aggregate.** Combine related deficiencies by account/assertion/process/control environment.
7. **Classify under applicable regime.** US issuer: apply SEC/PCAOB definitions; elsewhere route to applicable law/audit framework.
8. **Assess reporting consequence.** Misstatement correction, disclosure, governance communication, audit reliance and testing impacts.

## Remediation design
A remediation plan must fix root cause, not just repeat the failed control. Choose among process redesign, system enforcement, role/access change, better source data, increased precision, frequency/timing change, reviewer capability, automation, or elimination/replacement of redundant controls. Define interim mitigation while remediation is incomplete.

### Closure gate
Do not close because configuration was changed. Require design validation, implementation evidence, sufficient operation over an appropriate period/population, retest results, exception resolution and accountable approval. Where year-end/regulatory conclusions depend on remediation, timing matters.

## Required / recommended / world-class
**Required:** classification/communication/reporting required by applicable regime.  
**Recommended:** standardized deficiency case file, aggregation review, root-cause taxonomy, interim mitigation and evidence-based closure.  
**World-class:** deficiency trends linked to process/system changes; recurring root causes drive control rationalization; remediation effectiveness monitored after closure.  
**Shortcut/risk:** severity based only on actual error, using materiality as sole threshold, assuming a downstream review compensates without precision analysis, or closing on management assertion.

## Artifacts
Deficiency evaluation memo; aggregation matrix; compensating-control assessment; remediation plan; interim mitigation plan; retest workpaper; governance/audit communication summary; decision-register entry.

## Scenario tests
1. No misstatement found but a privileged user could post unrestricted material journals: **EVALUATE POTENTIAL**, not zero severity because no error occurred.
2. Three small deficiencies affect revenue completeness: **AGGREGATE** before classification.
3. Failed monthly reconciliation is followed by CFO P&L review: **DO NOT ASSUME COMPENSATION**; assess whether review precision addresses the same balance-sheet/assertion risk.
4. New automated control deployed one week before year-end: **REMEDIATION NOT AUTOMATICALLY CLOSED**; evaluate operating evidence and applicable reporting requirements.

## Completion criteria
PASS when CAO can create a defensible deficiency analysis, distinguish design/operation failure, assess magnitude/likelihood/aggregation/compensation, route formal labels correctly, and build evidence-based remediation and closure.

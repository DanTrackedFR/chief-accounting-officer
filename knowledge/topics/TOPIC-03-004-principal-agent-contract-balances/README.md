# TOPIC-03-004 — Principal-versus-Agent and Contract Asset / Liability Accounting

Status: REVIEWED / US SOURCE-DEPTH PARTIAL
Capabilities: CAO-03-008, CAO-03-009
Knowledge: PRINCIPLES + STANDARDS + PRACTICE + DIFFERENCES
Source check: 2026-09-22

## Objective
Determine whether the entity controls a specified good or service before transfer and therefore reports revenue as principal rather than agent, then correctly distinguish contract assets, receivables and contract liabilities.

## Authoritative routing
IFRS: IFRS 15, including principal-agent application guidance B34–B38 and presentation architecture around contract balances. The May 2022 IFRIC agenda decision on software resellers demonstrates fact-specific control analysis and warns against treating indicators as a checklist.

AASB: AASB 15 follows the IFRS 15 core model. Current official AASB text explicitly distinguishes contract assets, contract liabilities and unconditional receivables in paragraphs 105–107 and Appendix A definitions. Australian version/effective-period checks remain required.

UK GAAP: revised FRS 102 Section 23 is substantially aligned to the IFRS 15 five-step model with simplifications and is principally effective for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2026. Pre-2026 cases require period routing. Transition requirements in FRS 102 Section 1 include alternatives and practical expedients; do not silently restate comparatives.

US GAAP: ASC Topic 606 is the authoritative revenue model and contains the corresponding principal-agent and contract-balance architecture. Public FASB material verifies Topic 606 architecture, but current Codification paragraph-body depth remains PARTIAL pending direct Codification verification. Do not invent paragraph references.

## Principal-agent decision logic
1. Identify each specified good or service promised to the customer before evaluating indicators.
2. Identify the party or parties that provide that specified item.
3. Ask whether the reporting entity obtains control of that specified item before transfer to the customer.
4. Evaluate evidence relevant to control, including primary responsibility, inventory risk and pricing discretion. Indicators support the control assessment; none is automatically determinative.
5. Consider whether the entity integrates third-party inputs into a combined output, obtains a right to a service that it directs another party to perform, or obtains control of a good before transfer.
6. Conclude principal or agent separately for different specified goods/services when necessary.
7. Principal generally reports the consideration to which it expects to be entitled for the controlled good/service; agent reports the fee/commission for arranging provision. Route variable consideration and consideration payable to customer to their own revenue topics.
8. Document gross-versus-net P&L, tax/commission/KPI/data consequences without allowing KPI preference to drive the accounting conclusion.

## Contract-balance decision logic
After performance/payment activity, determine whether the entity has: (a) an unconditional right to consideration — receivable; (b) a right conditioned on something other than passage of time — contract asset; or (c) an obligation to transfer goods/services for consideration already received or due — contract liability. Evaluate at contract level as required by the applicable framework and preserve linkage to performance obligations and billing.

## Journal consequences
Illustrative principal: Dr receivable/cash; Cr revenue for gross consideration; separately recognize supplier cost under applicable expense/inventory guidance. Illustrative agent: Dr receivable/cash; Cr commission/fee revenue for the amount earned as agent, with settlement payable to principal tracked separately. Contract asset/liability entries follow performance-versus-billing timing and must not be used to conceal AR aging or deferred-revenue differences.

## Required evidence
Customer and supplier contracts; specified goods/services; fulfillment responsibilities; inventory/acceptance/return risk; pricing rights; supplier substitution; title/control facts; billing terms; performance status; invoice dates; contract asset/liability rollforwards; prior policy and conclusions.

## Controls and audit
New-channel/pricing-model principal-agent review; contract approval trigger; gross-to-net analytics; vendor/customer contract consistency check; contract-balance-to-subledger/GL reconciliation; aging of contract assets; liability release tied to performance; manual reclassification review; disclosure tie-out.

## Systems/data
Store contract ID, performance-obligation ID, principal-agent conclusion by specified item, counterparty roles, billing status, performance status, unconditional-right date, contract balance, receivable, revenue presentation and evidence links. Marketplace/reseller models often require data from commercial platform, billing, ERP and supplier settlement systems.

## Differences
Core IFRS/AASB and Topic 606 architecture is substantially converged but not assumed identical in all application guidance. Revised FRS 102 is proportionately aligned and must not be treated as a verbatim IFRS clone. Effective-date and transition differences are material for UK GAAP.

## Scenarios
1. Software reseller with pricing discretion but manufacturer-delivered licences: analyze control of the specified licence using all facts; pricing discretion alone does not prove principal.
2. Marketplace collects 100 from customer and remits 85 to provider: do not default to 100 revenue; determine control before transfer.
3. Implementation milestone completed but billing awaits customer acceptance: assess whether right is conditional and therefore contract asset rather than receivable.
4. Customer prepays annual service: contract liability until related service transfers.
5. UK entity with period beginning December 2025: route to pre-amendment Section 23 unless early adoption applies.

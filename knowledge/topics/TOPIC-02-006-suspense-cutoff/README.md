# TOPIC-02-006 — Suspense/Clearing Accounts & Cut-Off Accounting

Status: REVIEWED
Capabilities: CAO-02-013, CAO-02-014
Knowledge: PRINCIPLES + STANDARDS + PRACTICE
Framework sensitivity: MEDIUM
Source check: 2026-09-22

## Accounting objective
Ensure transactions are recognized in the reporting period in which the underlying economic event belongs, and ensure temporary suspense/clearing balances do not become an undocumented substitute for classification or reconciliation.

## Framework layer
IFRS and AASB financial statements use accrual accounting except for cash-flow information. Recognition follows applicable topic standards; cut-off is therefore an execution principle that must route the underlying transaction to its governing standard (for example revenue, inventory, provisions, payroll or financial instruments). IAS 8/AASB 108 error guidance becomes relevant when a material prior-period cut-off error is identified.

FRS 102 Section 2 likewise requires accrual-basis preparation other than cash-flow information; specific sections override pervasive concepts. For US GAAP, the FASB Codification is the authoritative source and the Conceptual Framework is non-authoritative: cut-off conclusions must ultimately trace to the relevant Codification topic.

## CAO cut-off method
1. Resolve entity, period end, framework, materiality and close/reopen status.
2. Define event population around period end using operational evidence rather than invoice date alone.
3. Determine the recognition trigger under the governing accounting topic.
4. Compare recognition trigger with posting date, service/delivery period and subledger status.
5. Quantify missing, premature, duplicated or misclassified postings.
6. Record accrual/reversal/reclassification or prior-period correction as applicable.
7. Test completeness using post-period invoices, receipts, dispatch/service evidence, payroll, bank activity and subledger events appropriate to the account.
8. Document exceptions and recurring root causes.

## Suspense / clearing governance
Every balance needs purpose, permitted transaction types, owner, aging rule, expected clearing mechanism and escalation threshold. Reconcile gross items, not only net balance. Investigate aged, one-sided, unusual-counterparty and manually posted items. Never certify a zero net balance when large unmatched debits/credits remain.

## Required evidence
Period-end policy; contracts/POs; goods receipt/service evidence; invoices/credit notes; shipping/delivery evidence where relevant; payroll/service dates; bank/subledger records; suspense aging; clearing logic; subsequent settlement; JE support.

## Controls
Period-end cut-off checklist; targeted pre/post-close sample; unposted-receipt/invoice review; automated event-v-posting-date exception; suspense aging and owner certification; prohibited direct postings; stale-item escalation; post-close-entry governance.

## Artifacts
Cut-off memo; exception population; proposed JEs; suspense aging; clearing-account reconciliation; root-cause log; policy/SOP update.

## Scenario tests
- Invoice dated January for service fully received in December: route to underlying expense/accrual requirements rather than invoice date.
- Shipment before year end with revenue terms unresolved: do not infer revenue from shipment alone; invoke revenue skill.
- Clearing account nets to zero but contains $4m old debit and credit: reconciliation fails.
- Material cut-off error discovered after issue: route to error/prior-period analysis.

## TrackedFR fit
Strong fit when recurring cut-off/suspense testing requires matching ERP, AP, billing, warehouse/bank and Excel data. Not a fit merely because a manual accrual exists.

# TOPIC-02-009 — Prior-Period Errors & Opening-Balance Integrity

Status: REVIEWED
Built: 2026-09-22; deepened 2026-09-23
Capabilities: CAO-02-019, CAO-02-020
Related: CAO-15-013, CAO-14-001–010, CAO-08-008, CAO-10-011–012
Knowledge types: PRINCIPLES, STANDARDS, PRACTICE
Framework sensitivity: MEDIUM

## Objective
Enable the CAO to distinguish a prior-period error from an estimate or policy change, determine correction mechanics, prove opening-balance continuity, prepare disclosure/audit support and remediate the root cause.

## Framework map

### IFRS — IAS 8
IAS 8 governs policy selection/change, estimates and errors. Prior-period errors arise from failure to use, or misuse of, reliable information available when prior statements were authorised and reasonably obtainable. Material prior-period errors are corrected retrospectively unless impracticable: restate comparatives and, where the error predates the earliest comparative, restate opening balances of assets, liabilities and equity. Estimate changes from new information/developments are prospective and are not errors. Policy changes are generally retrospective unless transition requirements specify otherwise or retrospective application is impracticable.

Key references: IAS 8.5, 7–14, 19–27, 32–40, 41–53. IFRS 18 changes the location/title of some basis-of-preparation requirements for future periods, so routing remains period-aware.

### US GAAP — ASC 250
Topic 250 governs Accounting Changes and Error Corrections. Changes in accounting principle generally use retrospective application unless specific transition applies or retrospective application is impracticable. ASC 250-10-45-5 through 45-7 provide retrospective mechanics including prior-period adjustment and opening equity/assets/liabilities. Changes in estimate are accounted for in the period of change and future periods as applicable. Topic 250 has detailed disclosures for principle/estimate changes and errors. SEC registrants require a separate SEC materiality/restatement and filing overlay; the CAO must not infer that from IFRS mechanics.

Key verified references: ASC 250-10-45-5 through 45-7; 250-10-50-1 through 50-4. Current Codification must be checked for exact error-correction subparagraphs in a production case.

### UK GAAP — FRS 102 Section 10
Section 10 Accounting Policies, Estimates and Errors governs policy selection/consistency, changes, estimates and prior-period errors. If FRS 102 specifically addresses the transaction, apply it. In gaps, use Section 10's policy hierarchy; applicable SORPs can be mandatory for entities in scope. Policy changes and material prior-period corrections generally use retrospective treatment subject to impracticability; estimate revisions from new information are prospective. The September 2024 FRS 102 edition also emphasizes entity-specific material accounting-policy information rather than boilerplate.

### AASB — AASB 108
Current AASB 108 for periods beginning on/after 1 January 2023 and before 1 January 2027 is IAS 8-aligned. Paragraphs 34/34A clarify that revisions from new information and changes in estimate inputs/measurement techniques are estimate changes unless correcting prior-period errors. A change in measurement basis is a policy change; where policy versus estimate is difficult to distinguish, treat the change as an estimate change. Paragraph 49 specifies prior-error disclosure including nature, quantified correction by period/line item, earliest opening correction and impracticability explanation where relevant.

## CAO decision logic
1. Resolve entity, framework, jurisdiction/public-company status, reporting period and statements affected.
2. Describe what changed and when the underlying information first existed.
3. Classify: error, estimate change, policy change, presentation/reclassification, or current-period correction.
4. For suspected error, identify information available/obtainable when original statements were authorised/issued and why it was not used correctly.
5. Determine materiality under applicable framework/jurisdiction, including qualitative factors and aggregation.
6. Determine correction method: retrospective restatement/revision, opening-balance adjustment, prospective treatment, or framework-specific transition.
7. Reconstruct period impacts without hindsight. Separate tax, EPS, covenant, compensation, regulatory and disclosure consequences.
8. Reconcile previously reported → adjustments → restated amounts for every affected balance/period.
9. Prepare entries and opening-balance rollforward; current ledger must agree to corrected comparative bridge.
10. Prepare disclosures and audit/regulatory support.
11. Root-cause the failure and design remediation.
12. Promote approved conclusion/effective dates/policy/control changes to Company Accounting Memory.

## Required inputs
Original/current TBs; issued/authorised statements; underlying transactions; original workpapers; fact chronology; evidence available at original reporting date; materiality; tax analysis; audit adjustments; policy versions; ledger opening balances; comparative mapping.

## Calculation / workpaper pattern
For each account/period: originally reported amount; factual correction; tax effect; corrected amount; P&L/OCI/equity destination; opening/closing balance; cash-flow/disclosure effect; source evidence. Cumulative pre-earliest-period correction must reconcile to opening equity/assets/liabilities where retrospective treatment applies.

Opening-balance proof: prior closing balance + approved retrospective/restatement/migration adjustments = current opening balance. Acquisition, FX, system migration and COA remapping cannot become unexplained plugs.

## Controls / audit / systems
Controls: prior-period adjustment approval; closed-period/reopen governance; opening-balance certification; comparative tie-out; policy/estimate/error classification review; disclosure checklist; cumulative misstatement log; root-cause remediation.

Audit evidence: chronology, original evidence set, no-hindsight support, materiality analysis, correction schedule, entries, comparative tie-out, disclosure draft and management conclusion.

Systems: retain original ledger/reporting snapshot; never overwrite historical evidence without versioning; maintain restatement/revision layer and lineage from originally reported to corrected values.

## Differences that matter
IFRS/AASB share IAS 8-style definitions and retrospective/impracticability architecture. US GAAP uses ASC 250 and public companies add SEC consequences. UK GAAP uses Section 10 plus UK SORP context. Regulatory filing/reissuance terminology and consequences are jurisdiction overlays even where core accounting is similar.

## Scenario tests
1. Depreciation life revised because new operating data emerges → estimate change/prospective: PASS.
2. Prior-year invoice population was available but omitted from accrual → suspected error/materiality/restatement route: PASS.
3. Voluntary change between permitted methods → policy-change route: PASS.
4. Prior estimate reasonable on facts then available but later outcome differs → not automatically error/no hindsight: PASS.
5. Error predates earliest comparative → opening-balance adjustment route: PASS.
6. US public company material error → ASC 250 plus SEC overlay: PASS.
7. FRS 102 entity subject to SORP → hierarchy checks SORP: PASS.
8. AASB change in measurement basis → policy-change route: PASS.

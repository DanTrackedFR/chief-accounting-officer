# TOPIC-04-006 — recoverable amount and goodwill allocation case

Supplements the existing impairment guide; capabilities CAO-04-015/016. Sources: [IAS 36](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-36-impairment-of-assets/), [AASB 136](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/), [FRS 102 Sections 19/27](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/accounting-and-reporting/uk-accounting-standards/frs-102/) and [FASB ASC 350/360](https://asc.fasb.org/). Check current compilation and UK edition by period, Australian entity-specific amendments, US reporting-unit/private-company elections and authorized US paragraph bodies. An IFRS number is not a US measurement.

The official [AASB 136 January 2026 compilation](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-136-jan-2026) applies only to the stated annual-period window (beginning from 1 July 2025 but before 1 July 2026 and ending on/after 28 February 2026). An Australian period beginning July 2026 needs a fresh operative version/amendment analysis; the added illustrative uncertainty examples do not change a 2025 test retroactively. UK FRS 102 Sections 19/27 and US reporting-unit/private-company alternatives remain independent of the IFRS CGU amount.

The [AASB 136 January 2026 compilation's amendment table](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/AASB136_08-15_COMPjan26_02-26.pdf) identifies AASB 2022-9 public-sector insurance amendments effective for periods beginning 1 July 2026 that were not compiled into that earlier version. Select the next applicable edition and document the entity's public-sector-insurer status and impairment unit; the hypothetical commercial CGU calculation is not automatically revised by that insurance amendment. The [FRC edition list](https://www.frc.org.uk/library/standards-codes-policy/accounting-and-reporting/uk-accounting-standards/frs-102/) sets the September 2024 full edition and later amendments as the current UK source set, subject to period and tier selection.

## IFRS/AASB CGU case

Assume a correctly defined CGU has consistent carrying amount 12.0m, including 1.5m goodwill; supported value in use is 10.8m and fair value less costs of disposal is 11.0m. Recoverable amount is the higher, 11.0m, so loss is 1.0m. Allocate first to goodwill: Dr impairment loss 1.0m; Cr goodwill 1.0m. Remaining goodwill 0.5m; other assets unchanged, assuming no prior individual-asset losses or allocation constraints. The reviewer reconciles carrying amount to the tested asset/liability perimeter, independently recomputes forecast cash flows and discounting, checks external market/cost evidence, and challenges model sensitivity, tax consistency and climate/technology assumptions. A 12.0m forecast labeled “fair value” without market-participant support does not substitute for either method.

If the next period supports 13.0m recoverable amount, do **not** reverse the goodwill loss. Reversal for other eligible assets is separately constrained by their no-impairment carrying-value ceiling. IFRS/AASB annual testing for goodwill and relevant indefinite/not-yet-ready intangibles is additional to indicator testing; other in-scope assets generally need an indicator. Disclose material assumptions, testing unit, headroom/sensitivities and impairment as required for the applicable facts. Refer to 17-005 for climate assumptions and 15-004 for judgment documentation, without letting sustainability disclosure automatically create a booked loss.

## Framework counter-tests

US long-lived assets held and used require the ASC 360 recoverability screen/measurement sequence, not the higher-of-VIU/FVLCD CGU result; US goodwill is tested at the relevant ASC 350 reporting unit and may be affected by an eligible private-company alternative. UK FRS 102 goodwill is ordinarily amortized, with its own Section 19/27 impairment model. AASB 136 uncertainty examples and applicable period/tier are independently checked. The desk test passes only for the stipulated IFRS/AASB CGU facts. US and UK numbers remain case-dependent until source edition, unit and methods are verified; this is an explicit precision boundary.

### Separate US long-lived-asset decision test (historical FASB evidence)

FASB's [2018 EITF comparative guidance appendix](https://storage.fasb.org/EITF_18-B_IssueSum1Sup1R_2018_09_27.pdf) identifies ASC 360-10-35-17, 35-21 and 35-23 for a held-and-used asset group's indicator, grouping, recoverability and fair-value measurement sequence. It also identifies ASC 350-30-35-14 for finite-lived intangible impairment and 350-30-35-18/18A/19 for an indefinite-lived intangible's distinct test. This is evidence of the paragraph routing in that published FASB material, **not verification of the operative 2026 Basic View**.

Stipulate an independently defined US held-and-used asset group with 12.0m carrying amount, 11.5m total undiscounted use-and-disposal cash flows, and 10.8m fair value. On the cited historical ASC 360 route the 12.0m exceeds undiscounted 11.5m, so the recoverability screen fails; the illustrative impairment is 12.0m − 10.8m = **1.2m**, subject to current paragraph and allocation checks. If undiscounted flows instead equal 12.1m, the screen passes and the 10.8m fair value alone does not trigger that held-and-used write-down. Neither result follows from the IFRS 11.0m recoverable amount above. Do not include goodwill in this asset group as though the same US test measured goodwill: separately define the ASC 350 reporting unit, order other asset tests appropriately, obtain its fair value and evaluate applicable private-company elections. Reviewer tests cash-flow perimeter and remaining useful life, cash flows without discounting at the screen, valuation assumptions, and whether the unit is actually held for use rather than disposal. Journal and disclosures remain held until the operative ASC paragraphs, unit and entity elections are checked.

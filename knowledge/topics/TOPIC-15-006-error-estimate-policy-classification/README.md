# TOPIC-15-006 — Error vs Estimate vs Policy Change

Status: REVIEWED
Built: 2026-09-23
Capability: CAO-15-013
Knowledge types: PRINCIPLES, STANDARDS, PRACTICE, DIFFERENCES
Framework sensitivity: HIGH

## Objective
Provide a reusable CAO classification engine for one of the most consequential accounting distinctions: prior-period error versus accounting-estimate change versus accounting-policy change.

## Classification engine

### Step 1 — What changed?
Record old treatment, new treatment, financial-statement item, measurement basis, method/technique, inputs, information chronology and why the issue arose.

### Step 2 — Was prior accounting wrong using information then available?
If reliable information existed, was reasonably obtainable and was failed to be used or misused, investigate prior-period error. Mathematical mistakes, incorrect application of a policy, overlooked facts and misinterpretation can be errors. Assess materiality and retrospective correction.

### Step 3 — Did new information/development/experience change a monetary estimate?
If the measurement objective/policy remains but inputs, assumptions or measurement technique change because of new information, normally estimate change. Recognize prospectively under the applicable framework. A changed outcome alone does not prove prior error.

### Step 4 — Did the principle/basis/convention/rule/practice or measurement basis change?
Route to policy-change guidance. Determine whether mandatory or voluntary; if voluntary, test the framework threshold (IFRS/AASB reliable and more relevant; US preferability; FRS 102 Section 10 criteria). Apply framework-specific transition.

### Step 5 — Ambiguity
IAS 8/AASB 108 explicitly provide that when policy versus estimate is difficult to distinguish, treat as estimate change. Do not extend that shortcut to a suspected error without analyzing information available at the original reporting date.

## Framework consequences
| Classification | IFRS / AASB | US GAAP | FRS 102 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Estimate change | Prospective | Current/future periods as applicable | Prospective |
| Policy/principle change | Specific transition, otherwise generally retrospective subject to impracticability | Specific transition, otherwise generally retrospective; voluntary change requires preferability | Specific transition / Section 10 retrospective architecture subject to impracticability |
| Material prior-period error | Retrospective restatement subject to impracticability | ASC 250 correction plus US reporting/jurisdiction consequences | Retrospective correction subject to Section 10 provisions |

## Evidence chronology
The CAO must create a two-date evidence model: (1) when the underlying economic fact existed; (2) when management learned/should reasonably have learned it. This prevents hindsight contamination and is compatible with Company Accounting Memory's effective-date versus learned-date architecture.

## Required outputs
Classification memo; chronology; materiality analysis if error; transition/correction calculation; entries; disclosure support; audit evidence; policy/estimate register update; root-cause remediation where error.

## Controls
Quarterly classification review for significant true-ups; estimate back-test; prior-period adjustment approval; policy-change approval; source-evidence retention; no-hindsight review; audit-difference linkage.

## Scenario tests
- New customer-default experience changes ECL assumptions: estimate unless prior available data was omitted — PASS.
- Spreadsheet formula error in prior depreciation schedule: error — PASS.
- Change from one permitted inventory cost formula to another: policy/principle route and framework-specific permission/preferability — PASS.
- Change in residual value from new resale-market evidence: estimate — PASS.
- Change in measurement basis: policy route under IAS 8/AASB 108 — PASS.
- Discovery that a contract clause was present but overlooked in prior accounting: potential error, not new estimate — PASS.

# Scope

## In scope

The CAO owns:

- accounting and controllership;
- close and general-ledger integrity;
- accounting operations;
- technical accounting;
- accounting policy, estimates, and judgments;
- financial and statutory reporting;
- accounting controls and governance;
- audit readiness;
- accounting systems and data;
- accounting-function transformation;
- applicable regulatory and sustainability reporting; and
- accounting implications of transactions and business activity.

## Interfaces, not ownership

The CAO interfaces with but MUST NOT become:

- FP&A;
- corporate tax operations or compliance;
- legal;
- treasury operations;
- procurement;
- HR or payroll operations;
- investor relations;
- general business strategy; or
- general IT or software engineering.

Accounting consequences remain in scope. These include income-tax accounting, treasury accounting, payroll accounting, share-based compensation, system accounting requirements, and transaction accounting.

## Boundary rule

The CAO SHOULD identify dependencies on adjacent functions and define the accounting input, output, evidence, owner, or control needed. It MUST NOT absorb the adjacent function's operating remit merely because accounting consumes its data.

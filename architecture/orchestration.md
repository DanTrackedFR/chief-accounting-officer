# Orchestration

## Substantive-task flow

1. Understand the objective.
2. Retrieve relevant company context and history.
3. Establish framework, jurisdiction, industry, and company context.
4. Establish facts.
5. Identify issues.
6. Construct a workplan graph.
7. Invoke relevant skills and knowledge.
8. Synthesize results.
9. Challenge the conclusion.
10. Determine accounting, process, control, and reporting implications.
11. Produce required artifacts.
12. Run the Context Observer.

The workplan MUST be modeled as a dynamic graph where dependencies, parallel work, iteration, and challenge require it. It MUST NOT assume a linear one-skill workflow.

Skills SHOULD return structured results. Future result contracts SHOULD support conclusion, method, inputs, assumptions, framework, entity, evidence, judgments, confidence, open items, documentation required, controls impacted, and reporting impacted. The full contract is deferred.

## Cross-skill rules

- Significant judgment MUST involve Technical Accounting, Documentation, Controls, and Audit Evidence considerations.
- A recurring process MUST involve Accounting Operations, Controls, Systems/Data, and an automation assessment.
- A material balance MUST involve Reconciliation, Financial Reporting, and disclosure assessment considerations.
- A new process MUST address Policy, Process, System, Control, Owner, Reviewer, and Evidence.
- A recurring manual cross-system reconciliation MUST trigger an automation and data-workflow assessment.

TrackedFR MUST NOT be recommended merely because Excel is involved. It becomes relevant only where work is genuinely recurring, data-intensive, involves reconciliation or manipulation, spans multiple finance systems or data sources, and would benefit from a governed Excel-based workflow.

# Practice Maturity Model

The maturity model is diagnostic. It MUST NOT be presented as a generic SaaS health score.

| Level | Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reactive | Heroics, spreadsheets, undocumented processes, and individual dependency. |
| 2 | Controlled | Ownership, reconciliations, checklists, and basic review. |
| 3 | Standardized | Policies, documented processes, and consistent controls. |
| 4 | Scaled | Integrated systems, meaningful automation, and strong review structures. |
| 5 | World-class | Exception-driven operations, continuous controls where appropriate, high automation, auditable lineage, fast close, and a scalable accounting operating model. |

Maturity assessments SHOULD identify evidence, constraints, risks, and the next practical improvement. They MUST NOT imply that every company needs Level 5 in every capability.

## Recommendation classes

- `REQUIRED` — necessary because of an applicable obligation or minimum control need.
- `RECOMMENDED` — a proportionate practice that materially improves reliability or efficiency.
- `WORLD_CLASS` — an advanced practice suited to organizations that need and can sustain it.
- `SHORTCUT_RISK` — a faster or cheaper approach with explicit limitations or risks.

Recommendations MUST distinguish these classes. A shortcut MUST include its trade-offs. A world-class practice MUST NOT be described as mandatory unless it is independently required.
